AlphaEvolve: A Coding Agent for Scientific and Algorithmic Discovery

Alexander Novikov, Ngân Vũ, Marvin Eisenberger, Emilien Dupont, Po-Sen Huang, Adam Zsolt Wagner, Sergey Shirobokov, Borislav Kozlovskii, Francisco J. R. Ruiz, Abbas Mehrabian, M. Pawan Kumar, Abigail See, Swarat Chaudhuri, George Holland, Alex Davies, Sebastian Nowozin, Pushmeet Kohli, Matej Balog

arXiv 2025 · 2025

Local PDFOnline

TL;DR

An evolutionary coding agent that has Gemini rewrite whole code files — prompting, evaluating, and re-seeding an island/MAP-Elites archive — to find better algorithms; it beat Strassen's 4×4 matrix-multiplication record after 56 years and shipped gains into Google's own training stack.

Abstract

In this white paper, we present AlphaEvolve, an evolutionary coding agent that substantially enhances capabilities of state-of-the-art LLMs on highly challenging tasks such as tackling open scientific problems or optimizing critical pieces of computational infrastructure. AlphaEvolve orchestrates an autonomous pipeline of LLMs, whose task is to improve an algorithm by making direct changes to the code. Using an evolutionary approach, continuously receiving feedback from one or more evaluators, AlphaEvolve iteratively improves the algorithm, potentially leading to new scientific and practical discoveries. We demonstrate the broad applicability of this approach by applying it to a number of important computational problems. When applied to optimizing critical components of large-scale computational stacks at Google, AlphaEvolve developed a more efficient scheduling algorithm for data centers, found a functionally equivalent simplification in the circuit design of hardware accelerators, and accelerated the training of the LLM underpinning AlphaEvolve itself. Furthermore, AlphaEvolve discovered novel, provably correct algorithms that surpass state-of-the-art solutions on a spectrum of problems in mathematics and computer science, significantly expanding the scope of prior automated discovery methods (Romera-Paredes et al., 2023). Notably, AlphaEvolve developed a search algorithm that found a procedure to multiply two $4 \times 4$ complex-valued matrices using $48$ scalar multiplications; offering the first improvement, after 56 years, over Strassen's algorithm in this setting. We believe AlphaEvolve and coding agents like it can have a significant impact in improving solutions of problems across many areas of science and computation.

Method

The user supplies an initial program with # EVOLVE-BLOCK-START/END markers, an evaluate() function returning scalar scores (maximized, possibly several), and optional prompt context. Loop: the prompt sampler draws a parent program plus diverse "inspiration" programs from the program database and assembles a rich prompt; an ensemble of Gemini 2.0 Flash (throughput) and Gemini 2.0 Pro (quality) returns edits as <<<<<<< SEARCH ======= >>>>>>> REPLACE diff blocks; apply_diff mutates the code; evaluators score the child, optionally through a cascade of increasingly hard test cases, with LLM-graded feedback (e.g. simplicity) as extra scores; survivors re-enter the database, which combines MAP-Elites with island-based populations to balance exploration and exploitation. Evaluation is asynchronous and parallel (on the order of 100 compute-hours per solution is affordable); meta-prompts themselves co-evolve in a second database. Programs can be evolved as raw solutions, constructor functions, or bespoke search algorithms.

Key results

  • Matrix multiplication: improves the state of the art for 14 of 54 tensor-decomposition targets, matches it in 38, falls behind in 2. Found the first rank-48 decomposition for multiplying two 4×4 complex-valued matrices — Strassen recursion gives 49, and AlphaTensor's rank 47 holds only over the field with 2 elements — the first improvement in this setting in 56 years.
  • Mathematics: on over 50 problems across 5+ branches, rediscovered the best known construction in 75% of cases and beat SOTA in 20%. Kissing number in 11 dimensions raised from 592 to 593 (a configuration of 593 non-overlapping unit spheres); Erdős minimum-overlap upper bound C5 improved 0.380926 → 0.380924; sums/differences lower bound C6 raised 1.1446 → 1.1584 via a set of size 54,265.
  • Data-center scheduling: a heuristic evolved against a fleet simulator and deployed fleet-wide continuously recovers on average 0.7% of Google's fleet-wide compute resources that would otherwise be stranded.
  • Gemini kernel engineering: the discovered tiling heuristic gives an average 23% kernel speedup over the expert-designed heuristic and a corresponding 1% reduction in Gemini's overall training time; optimization time dropped from several months of engineering to days.
  • Compiler/IR and hardware: 32% speedup on the FlashAttention kernel plus 15% on its pre/post-processing by editing XLA-generated IR, each change checked against reference code on randomized inputs; a Verilog rewrite removing unnecessary bits passed TPU verification and is integrated into an upcoming TPU.

Key advantages

  • Full-file evolution is the scale jump over FunSearch: instead of one Python function of 10–20 lines with a ≤20-minute single-CPU evaluator, AlphaEvolve evolves hundreds of lines across whole files in any language, with hours-long parallel accelerator evaluation, and thousands of LLM samples suffice where FunSearch needed millions.
  • Correctness is structural rather than trusted: every candidate is executed against an automated evaluator, so incorrect LLM suggestions are filtered out by construction. Mathematical constructions are provably valid, and the production heuristics are correct-by-construction because the scheduling score only re-ranks machines Borg already deemed eligible.
  • The paper walks the same problem at three abstractions — evolve the solution directly, evolve a constructor function, or evolve a search algorithm that finds it — and reports that the right choice is problem-dependent: constructor functions suit highly symmetric solutions, tailored search algorithms suit non-symmetric ones.
  • It is the clearest case of a model improving its own stack: the kernel heuristic accelerated Gemini's training, AlphaEvolve was instrumented to optimize the very LLM underpinning it, and the TPU circuit edit is described as Gemini's first direct contribution to TPU arithmetic circuits.

Limitations

  • The method only handles problems for which an automated evaluator can be devised — the paper names this its main limitation and puts tasks requiring manual experimentation (much of the natural sciences) out of scope.
  • It is compute-hungry and infrastructure-dependent: evaluation can absorb on the order of 100 compute-hours per candidate, and the ablations are averaged over just three independent runs and presented as budget curves rather than point estimates.
  • Some headline gains are marginal and can be overtaken: the Erdős overlap improvement is 0.380926 → 0.380924, and the paper itself notes a concurrent human result (Cohn et al.) reached 0.3284, after which re-running with their approach yielded 0.3216.
  • The production deployments are reported mostly without quantified gains (the TPU circuit rewrite and the scheduling heuristic's simulator-to-fleet transfer are described qualitatively), and the white paper defers many mathematics details to "an upcoming paper".
