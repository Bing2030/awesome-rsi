PAPER · stage 6 · Multi-Agent Self-Improvement

SOTOPIA-π: Interactive Learning of Socially Intelligent Language Agents

Ruiyi Wang, Haofei Yu, Wenxin Zhang, Zhengyang Qi, Maarten Sap, Graham Neubig, Yonatan Bisk, Hao Zhu

ACL 2024 · 2024

Local PDFOnline

TL;DR

Behavior cloning plus self-reinforcement on GPT-4-rated social interactions lifts Mistral-7B to GPT-4-level social goal completion on SOTOPIA (7.62 vs 7.62), improves safety, preserves MMLU — while revealing GPT-4 judges overestimate agents trained on GPT-4 ratings.

Abstract

Humans learn social skills through both imitation and social interaction. This social learning process is largely understudied by existing research on building language agents. Motivated by this gap, we propose an interactive learning method, SOTOPIA-$π$, improving the social intelligence of language agents. This method leverages behavior cloning and self-reinforcement training on filtered social interaction data according to large language model (LLM) ratings. We show that our training method allows a 7B LLM to reach the social goal completion ability of an expert model (GPT-4-based agent), while improving the safety of language agents and maintaining general QA ability on the MMLU benchmark. We also find that this training paradigm uncovers some difficulties in LLM-based evaluation of social intelligence: LLM-based evaluators overestimate the abilities of the language agents trained specifically for social interaction.

Method

Three automated steps. (1) Task generation: GPT-4 turns keywords sampled from Social Chemistry, Social IQa, and Normbank into scenarios with paired private goals (462 tasks from 510 prompts), reusing SOTOPIA's 40 character profiles — unfiltered, disjoint from eval tasks. (2) Data collection: behavior cloning (BC) data comes from GPT-4-agent vs GPT-4-agent interactions; self-reinforcement (SR) data from the Mistral-7B agent role-playing both characters — 100 tasks × 10 character-pair interactions per round. (3) Update: GPT-4 rates each agent's goal completion (0-10, the best human-correlated SOTOPIA-EVAL dimension). BC keeps the top-2 interactions per task per agent plus a rank-wise min(local, global) mean threshold; SR keeps the top 20% per task (ratio-based, replacing ReST's iteratively tuned thresholds). Filtered positive (context, history) → JSON-action pairs train Mistral-7B-Instruct via QLoRA SFT, sequentially BC then SR.

Key results

  • GPT-4-judged goal completion on SOTOPIA-hard: base Mistral-7B 3.25 → BC+SR 5.71, near the GPT-4 expert's 5.89; on all 90 tasks 5.07 → 7.62, exactly matching GPT-4's 7.62 (overall 2.33 → 3.44 vs GPT-4's 3.31).
  • Human evaluation is weaker: hard-task goal completion 2.89 (base) → 4.29 (BC+SR) vs GPT-4's 5.25; overall 1.04 → 1.95 (BC+SR vs GPT-4's 2.07, not significant, p=0.355).
  • The GPT-4-vs-human goal-score gap on hard tasks grows from 0.36 (base) to 1.42 (BC+SR): optimizing the LLM rating widens judge–human divergence.
  • Beyond goal completion (human, hard tasks), BC+SR over base: believability +2.05, relationship +1.91, social rules +1.11, overall +0.91 (all significant); knowledge −0.14, secret 0.00.
  • MMLU 5-shot preserved: BC+SR 48.57 vs base 49.21 (SR alone drops to 43.46). On a violence-refusal task, BC+SR's proceed-to-injure rate falls 100% → 44% (matching GPT-4), toxic words 3.6 → 0.9 (GPT-4: 0.3), and as the persuader character it prevents injury 11% → 100% with 2.9 alternative solutions vs GPT-4's 1.2.

Key advantages

  • Runs the BC + LLM-rated self-reinforcement recipe (ReST-style) on open-ended multi-turn social interaction — private goals, secrets, non-verbal actions — rather than Stable Alignment's single-turn QA, and a 7B model reaches the GPT-4 expert's goal-completion score under the same judge.
  • Ratio-based filtering (top 20% per task) removes ReST's iterative threshold tuning, enabling single-pass SR across tasks of mixed difficulty; one round of BC alone already surpasses the GPT-3.5-based agent (goal 4.82 vs 4.21, auto-rated hard tasks).
  • Side benefits without explicit alignment training: safety and toxicity improve on their probe task and MMLU is preserved, suggesting social-interaction skill is roughly orthogonal to general QA ability.
  • Documents reward hacking inside its own loop: training on GPT-4 ratings inflates GPT-4-judged scores faster than human scores — a concrete warning for any self-improvement pipeline that uses an LLM as its own reward.

Limitations

  • Headline parity holds only under GPT-4 judging; by human rating BC+SR stays below GPT-4 (4.29 vs 5.25 goal completion on hard tasks) and the judge–human gap grows 0.36 → 1.42 (the paper's own central caveat).
  • The method is offline and single-pass — no online RL or iteration (PPO left to future work); the expert is GPT-4, and human conversation data as a BC source is deferred.
  • Safety conclusions rest on a single custom social task (paper acknowledges); trained models still emit more toxic words than GPT-4 (0.9 vs 0.3), and SR alone degrades MMLU (43.46 vs 49.21).
  • Training tasks are auto-generated and deliberately unfiltered ('lower quality'), and human evaluation covers only 28 interactions per model on the 14 hard scenarios.

Where it fits the RSI arc

Extends self-improvement to the social dimension stage-6 multi-agent settings exercise: open-ended multi-turn role-play with private goals, secrets, and non-verbal acts. Its LLM-rater-as-reward + filtered self-training loop is self-play data curation for social skills — and the judge-vs-human divergence result is direct evidence of reward hacking in LLM-judged self-improvement.
