PAPER · stage 6 · Multi-Agent Self-Improvement

Improving Factuality and Reasoning in Language Models through Multiagent Debate

Yilun Du, Shuang Li, Antonio Torralba, Joshua B. Tenenbaum, Igor Mordatch

ICML 2024 · 2024

Local PDFOnline

TL;DR

Having multiple LLM instances propose, critique, and revise answers over debate rounds reliably beats single-agent generation and self-reflection — e.g. GSM8K 77.0 to 85.0% — and the resulting debate transcripts can be distilled back, creating a model self-improvement loop.

Abstract

Large language models (LLMs) have demonstrated remarkable capabilities in language generation, understanding, and few-shot learning in recent years. An extensive body of work has explored how their performance may be further improved through the tools of prompting, ranging from verification, self-consistency, or intermediate scratchpads. In this paper, we present a complementary approach to improve language responses where multiple language model instances propose and debate their individual responses and reasoning processes over multiple rounds to arrive at a common final answer. Our findings indicate that this approach significantly enhances mathematical and strategic reasoning across a number of tasks. We also demonstrate that our approach improves the factual validity of generated content, reducing fallacious answers and hallucinations that contemporary models are prone to. Our approach may be directly applied to existing black-box models and uses identical procedure and prompts for all tasks we investigate. Overall, our findings suggest that such "society of minds" approach has the potential to significantly advance the capabilities of LLMs and pave the way for further breakthroughs in language generation and understanding.

Method

Multiple copies of gpt-3.5-turbo-0301 independently answer the same prompt (zero-shot chain-of-thought) — only black-box generations, no weights or likelihoods. Each debate round, every agent receives the other agents' full responses concatenated as context plus a consensus prompt ("using the solutions from other agents as additional information, can you provide your answer?") and revises. Main configuration: 3 agents, 2 rounds; accuracy rises monotonically with agents and rounds (gains plateau past 4 rounds; 5+ agents need peers' responses summarized to fit context). "Stubborn" long-form prompts slow consensus and improve final answers; convergence is not guaranteed (debate is a multi-agent game) but agents almost always settle on one shared answer. Unlike majority voting, the model itself integrates the responses.

Key results

  • Arithmetic: 81.8±2.3% vs 67.0±4.7% single agent; self-reflection 72.1%, majority voting only 69.0% (3 agents, 2 rounds)
  • GSM8K: 85.0±3.5% vs 77.0±4.2% single agent (majority voting 81.0%); helps with and without zero-shot CoT
  • Chess move optimality (Stockfish pawn-score delta): 122.9±7.6 vs 91.4±10.6 single agent
  • Factuality: new 524-computer-scientist biography benchmark 73.8±2.3 vs 66.0±2.2; MMLU 71.1±4.6 vs 63.9 single — self-reflection drops MMLU to 57.7; chess move validity 45.2 vs 29.3
  • chatGPT+Bard debate solves 17/20 GSM8K vs 14 (chatGPT) and 11 (Bard) alone; persona initialization lifts MMLU 71.1 to 74.2

Key advantages

  • Makes debate between model copies a practical inference-time gain on black-box APIs: identical prompts and procedure across all six tasks, no gradient or likelihood access
  • Beats majority voting (the model integrates answers rather than tallying them) and peer critique beats self-reflection — which actually hurt factuality on MMLU (57.7 vs 63.9)
  • Not just answer amplification: many cases show all agents initially wrong yet converging on the correct answer; agents disagree on uncertain facts and drop them, and ease of persuasion may itself signal factual confidence
  • Explicitly frames debate transcripts as training data to distill back into the base model — a self-improvement loop — and introduces the biography factuality benchmark

Limitations

  • Computationally expensive: every round needs a full generation per agent, multiplying cost (paper's own §5), though framed as data generation for distillation
  • Convergence is not correctness: debates settle on single answers that can be wrong while agents confidently affirm them — models cannot express uncertainty, so shared errors survive (§5)
  • Long debates overflow attention: models "only focused on the most recent generations" of a long debate history
  • Small eval sets (~100 problems per task) and the biography metric uses chatGPT itself as judge; agents' agreeableness may be an artifact of RLHF instruction tuning
