AutoResearch: Insight In, Hallucination Out

Xiang Liu, Qumeng Sun, Bruno, Xiao Zhang, Jiahao Li, Haoyang Zhang

arXiv 2026 · 2026

Local PDFOnline

TL;DR

Two-stage autonomous-research system: Idea Generation grounds hypotheses in research signals plus domain knowledge through 3-model generation and 3-reviewer cross-validation; Idea Execution grounds conclusions in artifacts through fresh-context verification. RSICD mean Recall 32.84→34.69, with 5 audit-confirmed issue events vs 11–27 for four rival systems.

Abstract

Autonomous research systems are increasingly capable of executing long research workflows, yet automation alone does not ensure that the resulting process remains scientifically grounded. We introduce AutoResearch, a two-stage system that connects Idea Generation with Idea Execution to address both how research ideas are formed and how they are reliably established through experimentation. In Idea Generation, AutoResearch continuously integrates emerging research signals with accumulated domain knowledge, identifies transferable mechanistic insights, and uses multi-model generation and cross-review to produce grounded, testable research plans. In Idea Execution, coordinated agents decompose these plans into experiments, iteratively implement and diagnose them, and employ independent evidence-based review before accepting research conclusions. Across representative settings in cross-modal retrieval, systems optimization, and benchmark-driven machine learning, AutoResearch turns generated ideas into measurable progress, detects and corrects unreliable experimental results, and makes evidence-conditioned decisions to continue, revise, or terminate research directions. For example, on RSICD benchmark, an AutoResearch-generated idea improves mean Recall from 32.84 to 34.69, while recording only 5 audit-confirmed issue events compared with 11-27 for other autonomous research systems. These results demonstrate a research process in which meaningful insight is grounded before experimentation and conclusions are grounded before acceptance: Insight In, Hallucination Out.

Method

Idea Generation maintains two contexts: external signals harvested from papers, repositories, technical media and fast social platforms (X, Xiaohongshu) with source-aware quality priors, dedup and model-based screening; plus a curated domain knowledge base. Seeds are recast as mechanism-transfer problems H(s,d)={G_m⊕(s,K(d))} where each of M generators may return an explicit no-match instead of forcing a proposal. Idea Forge then runs three frontier models generating and three reviewers scoring, requiring at least two positive reviews on mechanism meaningfulness, methodological simplicity (isolate the effect) and testability, plus freshness and domain-consistency checks, emitting hypothesis h and plan p. Execution compiles p into a task graph over persistent state x_{k+1}=F(x_k,a_k,o_k), dispatches dependency-ready tasks concurrently, and routes non-PASS verdicts from a fresh-context critic (v_k ∈ PASS/PARTIAL/FAIL) into DIAGNOSE, REVISE or RERUN. Claims are admissible only as evidence items e_i=(a_i,o_i,r_i) with a persistent artifact; closure is CONTINUE/REVISE/SCALE/STOP.

Key results

  • RSICD bidirectional image–text retrieval: the Idea Forge method, introduced component by component under one fixed protocol, raises mean Recall 32.84 → 33.89 → 34.04 → 34.69 (total +1.85 mR), with each staged contribution remaining measurable.
  • Audit-confirmed issue events on RSICD: 5 for AutoResearch versus 11 R&D-Agent, 15 AutoResearchClaw, 18 Agent Laboratory, 27 The AI Scientist — the improvement comes with a comparatively reliable execution trace, not just a headline metric.
  • 1024×1024 FP32 matmul under a stated contract (<200 ms, relative error <1e-5 against two numerical references, run-to-run CV <20% over ten wall-clock trials): the pilot met speed and accuracy but failed stability (2/3 gates, CV unstable), triggering a diagnostic sweep over 8 cells that localized a timing bug — CPU time under multi-threaded BLAS conflated with wall-clock; the corrected, rerun baseline is 3.4 ms / 626 GFLOPS at 1024×1024 FP32, ~58× the required margin. AutoResearch logs 4 issue events vs 5 R&D-Agent, 5 Agent Laboratory, 7 AutoResearchClaw, 8 The AI Scientist.
  • Three Kaggle trajectories with evidence-conditioned closure: Titanic five-fold CV accuracy 0.822 → 0.843 (target ≥0.830) → scale-up; House Prices RMSLE 0.2008 → 0.1251 (target ≤0.120, not reached) → revise; Disaster Tweets F1 0.763 → 0.805 (target ≥0.835) → terminated with the negative result retained rather than searching for a positive one.
  • Throughput on one server (dual Intel Xeon Platinum 8563C, 98 cores/196 threads, 8× NVIDIA L20, 944 GiB): ~2584 candidate ideas in one week (7×24 h) of continuous operation → ~355 surviving multi-model review and filtering into the experimental queue → ~22 automatically executed experiments → ~14 empirically validated ideas.

Key advantages

  • Separates two grounding obligations that agent-scientist systems usually leave implicit: idea grounding (motivation traceable to context, a real transferable mechanism rather than superficial association, a testable central hypothesis) and evidence grounding (Accept(c) ⇒ ∃e∈E with Supports(e,c)=1; claim acceptance requires V(c,E_c,Γ_c)=PASS). The acceptance rule is checkable, which is what makes 'Hallucination Out' operational rather than rhetorical.
  • The critic is a fresh-context agent that never inherits the producer's reasoning trajectory — it gets only the hypothesis, plan, artifacts and evaluation criteria — so it must reconstruct validity from the research record. This explicit anti-anchoring design is the mechanism behind the low issue counts, and it is what the compared systems lack.
  • The matmul case is a concrete rejection of a favorable result: a pilot 58× faster than target was refused until its measurement process was independently justified, and the fix was to the measurement (CPU-time vs wall-clock), not to the claim. That is verification of process, not just of output.
  • Negative and inconclusive outcomes are first-class: termination can record a supported, falsified or inconclusive hypothesis, and Disaster Tweets is stopped below target with the negative result kept — research closure conditioned on accumulated evidence rather than on execution length or a required positive finding.

Limitations

  • The paper's own dependency list is the real boundary: the system still depends on external-signal coverage, the quality of accumulated domain knowledge, and 'the availability of explicit experimental criteria for verification'. Both corrected settings lean on machine-checkable contracts (timing/error/CV gates, Kaggle targets), so domains without such contracts — most open-ended discovery — are untested here.
  • The recursion is not closed: feeding verified evidence and outcomes back into the knowledge state (K_{t+1}=U(K_t,E_t,Y_t)) is explicitly future work. There is no demonstrated improvement across successive research cycles, so this is an RSI scaffold rather than a demonstrated self-improving research loop.
  • Evaluation is thin for the strength of the claims: three scenarios with single headline runs against four systems, and reliability is measured by the authors' own audit protocol of 'audit-confirmed issue events' with no multiple seeds, variance or inter-auditor agreement reported.
  • The front end is extremely lossy and prior-dependent — 2584 candidates → 355 queued → 22 executed → 14 validated — and idea quality is bounded by the curated knowledge base plus source priors the paper names explicitly (researchers and technical creators on X and Xiaohongshu as early signals ahead of literature search).

Where it fits the RSI arc

Stage 8 (Automated AI R&D): the mature generation/execution split with explicit acceptance rules rather than automation for its own sake. Baselines are The AI Scientist, Agent Laboratory, R&D-Agent and AutoResearchClaw; pairs with the autoresearch repo as its minimal industrial-scale counterpart, and with verification-gap surveys. Its future-work knowledge feedback is the RSI hook.
