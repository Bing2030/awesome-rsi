RRSI: Regularized Recursive Self-Improvement of Agent Harnesses

Peng Xia, Rujun Han, Zifeng Wang, Yanfei Chen, Yufan Zhang, Yoonho Lee, Chengsong Huang, Han Yu, Zhongying CuiZhu, Yifei Ming, Huaxiu Yao, Burak Gokturk, Tomas Pfister, Chen-Yu Lee

TL;DR

Regularizes harness self-improvement so it doesn't overfit the evolve split: proposal-side constraints (annealed edit budget, history-conditioned proposer, exploration redirection) and selection-side constraints (a critic that screens benchmark-specific edits, a noise-adjusted floor, a cost rule, and a pruner) favor reusable mechanisms over benchmark-specific ones — up to +14.1 points on the split it evolves against and up to +4.7 on the five out-of-distribution benchmarks, while the evolved harness runs on ~30% fewer policy tokens.

Abstract

An LLM agent's capability is largely magnified by its harness, namely the prompts, control flow, tooling, memory, and context management surrounding the frozen backbone model. Recent methods increasingly automate this process by iteratively proposing and selecting component-wise edits of an agent harness, practically establishing a form of recursive self-improvement (RSI) at the agent-system level. However, such recursive evolution may overfit by memorizing the training tasks, showing large in-distribution gains that shrink or even vanish on out-of-distribution benchmarks. We introduce Regularized Recursive Self-Improvement of Agent Harnesses (RRSI), which incorporates the principles of regularizations into harness self-improvement by constraining the evolution candidate proposal and selection. The proposer operates with a temporally annealed budget, limiting how many edits a candidate can bundle, and it encourages unexplored trajectories based on evolution history. The selector is equipped with a critic and a pruner: the critic screens benchmark-specific proposals, while the pruner, removes changes that are too small, too expensive, or no longer useful. Together these constraints favor reusable agent mechanisms over benchmark-specific ones or even noises. Across eight benchmarks spanning coding, agentic workspace and engineering design tasks, RRSI gains up to 14.1 points on the split it evolves against and up to 4.7 points on the five out-of-distribution benchmarks, while producing a harness that runs on 30% fewer policy tokens than the unregularized evolution. Code is available at https://github.com/google-research/rrsi and project page is https://regularized-rsi.com/.

Method

A harness-evolution loop where the edit space stays open (prompts, control flow, config, context, tools, skills, memory, sub-agents) but the search is regularized. Proposal side: an annealed budget b_t caps how many edits one candidate can bundle; the proposer is conditioned on the full edit history so a falsified hypothesis isn't redrawn; a stalled run is redirected toward components it has never exercised. Selection side: a critic screens every candidate for suite-specific logic before evaluation; a noise-adjusted floor blocks gains within evaluation variance; a cost rule requires added inference tokens to be paid for by measured gain; and components that stop helping are pruned. Each candidate is drafted, screened and evaluated in its own git worktree, and accepting one fast-forwards the branch — the incumbent is always a commit.

Key results

  • Coding (Claude Opus 4.8 policy): Terminal-Bench 2.1 +6.0 (74.2 → 80.2) on the evolve split; SWE-bench Verified +1.8 (82.0 → 83.8) out-of-distribution.
  • Agentic workspace: Harvey LAB +1.1 evolve; JobBench +4.7, GDPval +3.5, APEX-Agents +3.7 out-of-distribution.
  • Engineering design: EngDesign +4.9 evolve; Frontier-Eng +4.3 out-of-distribution.
  • Not tied to one policy family: with Gemini 3.5 Flash the coding instance goes 64.6 → 78.7 on Terminal-Bench 2.1 and 76.8 → 79.0 on SWE-bench Verified.
  • The evolved harness runs on ~30% fewer policy tokens than unregularized evolution.

Key advantages

  • Isolates the failure mode the harness-evolution line papers over, and measures it: on the agentic workspace instance all four prior methods beat H_0 on the evolve split (Meta-Harness 93.0, HarnessX 91.8, TTHE 91.1, AHE 90.7 vs H_0 89.4) yet out of distribution only Meta-Harness clears H_0 (40.6 vs 39.7, +0.9), HarnessX ties it, and AHE and TTHE end below the harness they started from (TTHE by 1.7). RRSI posts the smallest evolve gain of any evolved harness (90.5) and the only OOD average clearing H_0 by more than a point (43.6 vs 39.7) — the trade the regularizers are designed to make.
  • The ablation makes the mechanism causal rather than asserted: removing both regularizer groups yields the highest evolve-set score of any arm (92.8 vs RRSI's 90.5) while the OOD average falls to 40.3, within a point of the unevolved H_0, at 3.80M policy tokens per trial against RRSI's 2.42M; removing only the acceptance constraints raises evolve to 91.5, drops OOD to 41.0 and adds ~half the token cost, while removing only the proposal constraints costs 0.2 evolve but 1.7 OOD — steering where the search looks matters even when nothing is rejected.
  • Transfer and cheapness are obtained together, which no baseline manages: RRSI's final harness spends 2.42M policy tokens per trial on the evolve split vs 3.80M for unregularized evolution and 3.82M for AHE (58% more for 4.4 points less out of distribution), and runs 26.3 steps per trial against 27.3–34.6 for the prior methods — the gain-dependent cost rule (ΔC ≤ β0 + β1·ΔS) refuses growth that is not paid for at proposal time, and L1-style pruning deletes structure that stopped paying later.
  • The regularizers are policy- and grader-agnostic: the same loop improves all six held-out splits with no regression anywhere, replicates under a second policy family (Gemini 3.5 Flash: Terminal-Bench 2.1 64.6 → 78.7, +14.1; SWE-bench Verified 76.8 → 79.0), carries an evolved harness onto a backbone that never took part in the search (Gemini 3.1 Flash Lite 11.2 → 14.6, +30.4% relative), and holds under deterministic graders where no judge can be flattered (EngDesign +4.9; Frontier-Eng +4.3 Medal points, +24.3% relative).

Limitations

  • Regularization narrows but does not close the OOD gap — up to +4.7 points out-of-distribution versus up to +14.1 on the evolve split, so in-distribution overfitting is reduced, not eliminated.
  • Depends on capable LLM roles (proposer, analyst, critic, all Claude Opus 4.8) and a strong frozen policy to search against (Claude Opus 4.8 / Gemini 3.5 Flash); the one weaker backbone tested (Gemini 3.1 Flash Lite) only receives an already-evolved harness — it never drives evolution, and the paper's own limitations note that broader validation across architectures, tool ecosystems and longer runs is needed.
  • Each instance is a hand-built Domain adapter with per-domain hyperparameters (the noise band is calibrated per instance), so porting to a new domain is engineering work, not automatic.
  • The critic is an LLM regex-deny + review screen for leakage, not a guarantee of non-leakage; the noise floor is an empirical estimate.

Where it fits the RSI arc

The closest published match to rsif's non-regression discipline, applied at the harness layer. RRSI's noise-adjusted acceptance floor, cost rule, and pruner are the direct analogs of rsif's val gate, budget constraints, and archive promotion/rollback — and its critic is the analog of rsif's held-out gates and canaries, all aimed at the same failure mode rsif engineers against: gains that memorize the training surface instead of generalizing. Where rsif evolves the agent's artifacts under executed gates, RRSI evolves the harness's edit space under measured-score gates.
