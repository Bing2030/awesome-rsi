AI4AI at Test-Time: Strong-to-Weak Capability Transfer via Harnesses

Cheng Qian, Wenting Zhao, Liangwei Yang, Heng Wang, Jielin Qiu, Heng Ji, Silvio Savarese, Huan Wang, Shelby Heinecke

arXiv 2026 · 2026

Local PDFOnline

TL;DR

A strong builder model writes an inference-time scaffold for a frozen weaker target: across 57 runs on 3,900 Theory-of-Mind items, GPT-5.4-mini rises from 0.488 to 0.763 mean and 0.912 best, with no weight updates — gains come from deterministic offloading, routing and format enforcement.

Abstract

Recent work on distillation transfers the capabilities of large models to smaller ones often by updating the latter's parameters, through teacher forcing, on-policy distillation, and related training-time methods. In this paper, we ask whether such transfer can instead occur at test time. We study strong-to-weak scaffolding: whether a stronger builder model can construct inference-time harnesses that help a weaker target model solve tasks more reliably without any parameter updates. Using four representative Theory-of-Mind benchmarks, each builder model uses 5% of the data as a validation set to iteratively refine its harness over multiple rounds, after which the finalized harness is evaluated on the full test set. Empirically, this form of test-time capability transfer is highly effective, nearly doubling average target-model performance from 0.49 to 0.91. Our analysis shows that the gains come primarily from offloading unstable model reasoning into deterministic code, benchmark-specific routing, and strict answer-format enforcement, rather than from encouraging the target model to reason more extensively or sample more broadly. We further find that builder-model reasoning effort improves harness quality monotonically, platform effects are modest relative to the builder model's own capability, and weaker target models receive the largest gains. These results suggest that inference-time harness design is an important complement to conventional training-time distillation, enabling strong models to transfer cognitive structure to weaker models without retraining.

Method

Four ToM benchmarks (BigToM 1200, Hi-ToM 1200, MMToM-QA 600, MuMA-ToM 900 = 3900 hidden test items) are split: 195 items (5%) become a labeled validation set V, the rest are hidden. A strong builder model M_build is dropped into an agentic coding platform (Cursor, Claude Code, or GPT Codex) with a workspace holding only a rule file R, a demo file C_demo showing how to call the frozen target M_tar, and V. The scaffold — free-form Python, no fixed architecture — must expose one entry point f_S(x; M_tar). Each round: read R/C_demo/V, write or revise the scaffold, run it on V (each validation evaluation costs 1 against a secondary scoring criterion), then fold the new scaffold, its accuracy, and the list of misclassified items E_k back into the workspace. Loop until submission; a human then runs the frozen entry point once on T.

Key results

  • Main: across all 57 scaffolded GPT-5.4-mini runs the mean macro-average is 0.763 vs the 0.488 vanilla baseline (+0.275), and 100% of runs and all 11 builder configurations beat baseline; the best run (GPT-5.5 on GPT Codex) reaches 0.912, +0.423 (86.7% relative), exceeding the no-scaffold raw GPT-5.4 (0.619) and GPT-OSS-120B references and approaching the human-designed UserHarness (0.939).
  • Mechanism: accuracy tracks the determinism fraction — the share of the 3,900 items answered wholly by code/rules (Pearson r = 0.72, n = 18) — whereas scaffold code size barely does (r ≈ 0.22). Offloadability is benchmark-specific: BigToM 0.94, Hi-ToM 0.51, MMToM-QA 0.44, MuMA-ToM 0.36. The best scaffold fixes 1,717 baseline errors and breaks 105 (paired McNemar χ² = 1424.4, p < 10⁻⁴).
  • Technique prevalence over the 57 GPT-5.4-mini runs: format enforcement 57/57, greedy/temperature control 56/57, benchmark routing 54/57, forced CoT 45/57, polarity-negation logic 45/57, token-budget tuning 43/57, hybrid fallback 34/57, deterministic solver 31/57, structured extraction 29/57, few-shot 12/57, verification/arbiter 7/57, self-consistency 3/57. Best-powered accuracy associations: polarity/negation +0.090, structured extraction +0.055, few-shot +0.042, hybrid fallback +0.040, deterministic solver +0.026.
  • Builder effort and validation budget: Opus-4.7 pooled accuracy rises monotonically with reasoning tier — 0.711 low, 0.793 medium, 0.807 high, 0.856 extra-high (Spearman ρ = 0.77; extra-high vs high p = 0.013, vs low p = 0.002) while scaffold size grows from ~510–650 LOC to ~1,000–1,300 LOC. Builders used a median of 5 validation evaluations (mean 4.9, range 2–15); best validation accuracy tracks final full-set accuracy at r = 0.96 with only a 0.021 optimism gap, while the number of iterations does not (r = 0.17).
  • Headroom law: scaffolding lifted the weak target GPT-5.4-mini by +0.262 (0.488 → 0.750) but the stronger Gemini-3.5-flash by only +0.110 (0.761 → 0.871), with uplift predicted by the target's remaining headroom 1 − baseline (Pearson r = 0.75). Gemini's gain is 96% concentrated on BigToM (+0.42, 0.49 → 0.92), and 9/20 of its matched builder×benchmark cases regress (Hi-ToM −0.04, MuMA-ToM −0.02) versus 0/20 for GPT-5.4-mini.

Key advantages

  • Establishes test-time capability transfer as a measured regime rather than an analogy to distillation: the transfer channel is the harness, the target is never trained, and the builder pays a one-time reasoning cost to 'compile' task structure — the explicit mirror image of work that distills a harness into weights.
  • Isolates the mechanism instead of asserting it. Determinism fraction correlates with accuracy (r = 0.72), item-level effects are strongly asymmetric (1,717 baseline errors fixed vs 105 broken; top-8 scaffolds repair 83% of baseline-wrong items and break only 7% of baseline-correct ones), and two competing explanations are ruled out with numbers: more validation probing (r = 0.17) and longer/more sampling do not predict quality.
  • Clean factorial design with real controls: 72 runs over builder × platform × target × 3 repeats, plus no-scaffold baselines, a human-designed UserHarness reference, and a self-scaffolding control (GPT-5.4-mini building for itself already gains +0.17 to +0.22, but stronger builders reach +0.27 to +0.31), so the 'builder capability matters' claim has a floor to compare against.
  • Ships an actionable recipe with the caveats attached: strongest available builder, high reasoning effort, only ~5 validation evaluations, deterministic offloading for provable sub-tasks, and — because top scaffolds repair complementary error sets (their union covers 97% of baseline errors) while run-to-run variance stays around sd 0.036 — building two or three scaffolds and selecting or ensembling by validation score.

Limitations

  • Scope is four text-format Theory-of-Mind benchmarks only. The authors frame ToM as a representative testbed chosen for its mix of compilable structure and hard residual reasoning, and explicitly leave other families — different mixtures of symbolic structure, ambiguity and open-ended reasoning — to future work.
  • Part of the headline gain is benchmark-structure exploitation rather than general reasoning: on BigToM the automated scaffold reaches 1.00 vs the human harness 0.95 by compiling the observed/unobserved distinction into rules. The paper argues this is legitimate under a validation-only, no-hard-coding rule set, but it is not evidence of improved reasoning.
  • A residual floor remains where structure resists compilation: Hi-ToM accuracy falls from 0.999 at recursion order 0 to 0.700 at order 4, deception reduces it further (0.829 → 0.772), MMToM-QA qtype 2.1 sits at 0.680, and MuMA-ToM social-goal / belief-of-goal stay at 0.872 / 0.880 while plain belief questions reach 0.985.
  • Scaffolding can backfire and the build is not deterministic: on the already-strong Gemini-3.5-flash every builder regressed on at least one benchmark (9/20 matched cases), and while mean within-cell sd is 0.036 the worst cell has a 0.201 repeat range, concentrated in brittle deterministic-solver scaffolds. Technique-level attribution is explicitly associational, since techniques co-occur heavily.
