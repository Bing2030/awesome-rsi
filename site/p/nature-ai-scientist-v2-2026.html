Towards End-to-End Automation of AI Research (The AI Scientist-v2)

Chris Lu, Cong Lu, Robert Tjarko Lange, Yutaro Yamada, Shengran Hu, Jakob Foerster, David Ha, Jeff Clune

Nature 2026 · 2026

Local PDFOnline

TL;DR

The AI Scientist-v2 automates the full research loop — ideation, code, experiments, figures, LaTeX manuscript and automated peer review — and one fully AI-generated paper passed peer review at an ICLR 2025 workshop, though only 1 of 3 submissions cleared the bar.

Abstract

Article Towards end-to-end automation of AI research https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-026-10265-5

Method

Four sequential phases. (1) Ideation: iteratively grow an archive of research directions, each a title plus hypothesis plus plan scored 1-10 on interestingness, novelty and feasibility; Semantic Scholar API and web tools discard ideas too close to existing literature (<=10 rounds). (2) Experimentation, in two modes: template-based extends a human code template with Aider, running up to five planned experiments with auto-debugging (four reattempts, 7,200 s timeout); template-free replaces the linear workflow with a parallelized agentic tree search (Claude Sonnet 4 writes plan and code, o3 critiques code, GPT-4o critiques plots) across four gated stages - preliminary investigation, hyperparameter tuning, research agenda, ablations - with 1 h nodes and hyperparameter, ablation, replication, aggregation variants; GPT-4o best-first search picks nodes and the best leaf seeds the next stage. (3) Write-up: o1 fills a LaTeX template from the experimental journal, Semantic Scholar supplying related work. (4) o4-mini reviews the compiled PDF.

Key results

  • Workshop peer review: of three AI-generated manuscripts submitted to the ICLR 2025 ICBINB workshop (43 papers reviewed, 70% acceptance rate), one averaged 6.33 (individual scores 6, 7, 6), above the acceptance threshold and among the top 45% of submissions; organizers said it would likely have been accepted. All were withdrawn per a pre-established protocol.
  • Automated Reviewer (o4-mini, ensemble of five reviews plus an area-chair meta-review) on ICLR/OpenReview data: balanced accuracy 0.69 +/- 0.04 versus 0.66 for human NeurIPS reviewers, F1 0.62 +/- 0.09 versus 0.49 inter-human, AUC 0.69 versus 0.65, FNR 0.17 versus 0.52.
  • Contamination check: balanced accuracy drops from 0.69 on 1,000 pre-cutoff papers (2017-2024) to 0.66 +/- 0.03 on the clean 2025 set, still comparable with humans; two-sample z-tests show no significant difference (P = 0.319 pre-cutoff, P = 0.921 post-cutoff) while a bootstrap test favors the automated reviewer on F1 (P < 0.001).
  • Model recency: Automated Reviewer paper scores rise with foundation-model release date (fit R = 0.517, P < 0.00001; n = 6 template-free points, n = 3 template-based points).
  • Compute scaling: spending more test-time compute in the agentic tree search (5 to 30 experimental nodes, n = 30 papers per budget point) improves paper scores.

Key advantages

  • First demonstration that a fully AI-generated manuscript can navigate a real, blind peer review at a top-tier venue, with no human modification of the ideas, code or text anywhere in the pipeline.
  • Removes the v1 template: the template-free mode writes its own starting code and swaps the linear plan for a parallelized agentic tree search with stage gates and specialized node types, so it can scale exploration with test-time compute.
  • Ships the evaluation half of the loop: an Automated Reviewer whose agreement with human decisions (F1 0.62, AUC 0.69) matches inter-human agreement, including after the model knowledge cutoff, making quality measurable at scale.
  • Supplies trajectory evidence rather than a single point: paper quality tracks both base-model release date and per-paper compute, so the pipeline should improve as models and inference budgets improve.

Limitations

  • Consistency is not there: only 1 of 3 submissions passed, the ICBINB workshop accepts 70% versus 32% for the ICLR 2025 main conference, and the authors' own internal review found none of the papers met a main-conference bar.
  • Failure modes named in the paper: naive or underdeveloped ideas, incorrect implementations of the stated idea, shallow methodological rigour, experimental implementation errors, duplicated figures across main text and appendix, and hallucinations including inaccurate citations.
  • Scope is computational machine-learning research only, and the workshop run relied on human filtering of the most promising ideas, code and manuscripts at each stage rather than a fully unattended pipeline.
  • Ethical and societal risks the paper raises: overwhelming the peer-review system, inflating research credentials, misusing others' ideas, and doing unethical or dangerous experiments; all submissions were withdrawn because no norms for disclosing AI-generated research exist yet.

Where it fits the RSI arc

Stage 8 (Automated AI R&D): the reference system and shared baseline of the stage - the complete ideation to execution to write-up to review loop, now de-templated and peer-reviewed.
