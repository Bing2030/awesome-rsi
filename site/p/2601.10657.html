PACEvolve: Enabling Long-Horizon Progress-Aware Consistent Evolution

Minghao Yan, Bo Peng, Benjamin Coleman, Ziqi Chen, Zhouhang Xie, Shuo Chen, Zhankui He, Noveen Sachdeva, Isabella Ye, Weili Wang, Chi Wang, Ed H. Chi, Fernando Pereira, Wang-Cheng Kang, Derek Zhiyuan Cheng, Beidou Wang

arXiv 2026 · 2026

Local PDFOnline

TL;DR

PACEvolve diagnoses three failure modes of LLM-driven evolutionary search — context pollution, mode collapse, weak collaboration — and fixes them with hierarchical idea memory, momentum-triggered backtracking, and self-adaptive cross-island crossover, reaching state-of-the-art on symbolic regression, KernelBench, and Modded NanoGPT.

Abstract

Self-evolving agents powered by Large Language Models (LLMs) have emerged as a promising direction across diverse domains, including code optimization and scientific discovery, yet their core failure modes remain underexplored. Through a comprehensive empirical study, we identify that the model's reasoning becomes anchored to the local context of current hypotheses, overemphasizing low-level details while neglecting the broader search landscape. As a result, such agents become prone to context pollution and mode collapse, repeatedly revisiting flawed hypotheses and converging on suboptimal solutions. To address this challenge, we propose Progress-Aware Consistent Evolution (PACEvolve), a systematic framework for governing agent memory and search dynamics. PACEvolve overcomes these limitations through three key techniques: (1) Hierarchical Context Management (HCM), which structures historical trajectories while dynamically pruning branches to preserve a high-signal memory state; (2) Momentum-Based Backtracking (MBB), which monitors optimization progress to escape local minima; and (3) a self-adaptive Collaborative Evolution policy (CE) that balances intra-trajectory refinement with inter-trajectory knowledge transfer. By decoupling high-level idea generation from low-level code evaluation, PACEvolve maintains a global view of search momentum and achieves state-of-the-art results across complex evolutionary benchmarks.

Method

From 200+ 1000-step evolution runs the authors find append-only history contexts saturate with failed trials (context pollution), agents anchor to locally similar hypotheses (mode collapse), and static periodic crossover wastes parallel compute (weak collaboration). PACEvolve evolves candidate solutions (equations, GPU kernels, training code) via three mechanisms. HCM decouples idea generation from selection over a persistent idea pool (caps: 5 ideas, 20 hypotheses each): an LLM classifier merges conceptually duplicate ideas, caps trigger summarization and LLM pruning, and a permanent failure log preserves consistency by blocking rediscovery of dead ends. MBB computes scale-invariant Relative Progress R_t (fraction of remaining gap G_t = s_t − r closed), smoothed into momentum m_t by EWMA (β=0.85); when m_t < 0.001 the trajectory reverts to an ancestor state drawn from a power-law (α=1.5), resetting context. CE then samples backtrack vs. crossover from other islands with probability proportional to absolute-progress-based weights.

Key results

  • LLM-SR nonlinear-oscillator symbolic regression (Log10 NMSE, 10 runs × 1000 iterations, Gemini 2.5 Pro): PACEvolve-Multi best −8.24 / P75 −7.64 / mean −6.11 vs ShinkaEvolve −6.35 / −5.92 / −5.35, OpenEvolve −7.11 / −5.79 / −5.40, and non-LLM uDSR best −4.06; Multi finds 3 solutions below log10 NMSE −8.
  • KernelBench (16 kernels, 1000 iterations each, A100 40GB): beats the best KernelBench-leaderboard kernel on all 16, beats ShinkaEvolve on all 16 (PACE-Multi ≥ Single on 13/16, i.e. 81.25%), matches-or-beats OpenEvolve on 15/16 and CodeEvolve/ShinkaEvolve on 14/16; up to 17.38× over PyTorch (LayerNorm, 10.1ms → 0.581ms), though it loses to PyTorch on MLP/MatMul.
  • Modded NanoGPT (8×H100, Gemini 3 Pro): cuts time to val loss 3.28 on FineWeb from the heavily optimized v40 baseline 142.8s → 140.2s via data sharding/preloading, U-shaped skip-connection init (0.8→0.4→0.8), hyperparameter tuning, and dynamic context scheduling — on a benchmark already optimized from ~2700s (v1) to 142s.
  • Ablation (10 runs each): HCM lifts mean/best but worst runs still stall (a locally-optimal idea is never erased); adding MBB eliminates stalled trajectories yet slightly hurts best runs (exploration tax); adding CE recovers top-run performance while keeping the floor — full stack needed.

Key advantages

  • Real diagnosis, not anecdote: 200+ 1000-step runs show trajectories that fail to find low-NMSE solutions in the first few iterations rarely recover, with large variance across independent runs (Fig. 2) — grounding context pollution and mode collapse in measured search dynamics.
  • Each failure mode gets a matched, ablation-isolated fix: HCM's bi-level pruning cleans context, and MBB's progress-aware trigger removes the worst-run tail that context erasure alone cannot (§4.3).
  • The scale-invariant Relative Progress metric lets one stagnation trigger work across tasks without task-specific thresholds, unlike fixed-schedule resets in ShinkaEvolve/FunSearch-style loops that ignore search state.
  • Same scaffold achieves SOTA across three distinct domains — scientific equation discovery, GPU kernel optimization, and full-stack distributed training engineering — suggesting the recipe transfers.

Limitations

  • By the paper's own ablation, backtracking 'slightly affected the best-performing processes' (exploration taxes exploitation), and CE only helps 'provided that at least one island discovers a promising direction' — joint all-island stagnation remains a risk.
  • No explicit limitations section; the authors flag KernelBench/Modded-NanoGPT world knowledge as 'questionable' (Table 5) since optimization strategies likely sit in LLM pretraining corpora, so gains may partly reflect retrieval rather than search.
  • Tasks need smooth rewards and cheap evaluation per the paper's own criteria; Modded NanoGPT needs 8 H100s and its gain is incremental (142.8s → 140.2s, conceded as 'this improvement looks incremental').
  • Mechanism assumes a known metric lower bound r and tuned constants (β=0.85, ε_rel=0.001, caps 5/20, power-law exponent 1.5); a sweep (Fig. 5) reports robustness but values are still benchmark-derived.

Where it fits the RSI arc

Stage-9 prerequisite: open-ended evolution stalls without memory governance — long-horizon search needs structured, pruned idea memory plus a progress signal that decides when to abandon a line of work. Its diagnosis-first methodology (measure failure modes across 200+ runs, then derive the scaffold) pairs naturally with PACE-style diagnosis in this catalog.
