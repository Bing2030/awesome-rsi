Large Language Models as Optimizers

Chengrun Yang, Xuezhi Wang, Yifeng Lu, Hanxiao Liu, Quoc V. Le, Denny Zhou, Xinyun Chen

ICLR 2024 · 2024

Local PDFOnline

TL;DR

OPRO describes an optimization task in natural language and has an LLM iteratively propose new solutions from a meta-prompt listing past solutions and their scores; on GSM8K it finds prompts beating "Let's think step by step" by 8.4 points.

Abstract

Optimization is ubiquitous. While derivative-based algorithms have been powerful tools for various problems, the absence of gradient imposes challenges on many real-world applications. In this work, we propose Optimization by PROmpting (OPRO), a simple and effective approach to leverage large language models (LLMs) as optimizers, where the optimization task is described in natural language. In each optimization step, the LLM generates new solutions from the prompt that contains previously generated solutions with their values, then the new solutions are evaluated and added to the prompt for the next optimization step. We first showcase OPRO on linear regression and traveling salesman problems, then move on to our main application in prompt optimization, where the goal is to find instructions that maximize the task accuracy. With a variety of LLMs, we demonstrate that the best prompts optimized by OPRO outperform human-designed prompts by up to 8% on GSM8K, and by up to 50% on Big-Bench Hard tasks. Code at https://github.com/google-deepmind/opro.

Method

The optimization task (objective, constraints, desired output format) is written in natural language and becomes the meta-prompt. The meta-prompt has two parts: a task description carrying ~3 exemplars randomly drawn from the training set, and the optimization trajectory — past solutions paired with their scores, sorted ascending and truncated to the best 20 to fit the context window. Each step the optimizer LLM is sampled 8 times at temperature 1.0 to emit 8 candidate solutions; each is scored by a black-box evaluator, then the solution-score pairs are appended and the trajectory re-sorted. The loop halts when no better solution appears or a step budget is hit (200 steps for prompt optimization). Scoring uses temperature 0, and the scorer LLM may differ from the optimizer LLM. Nothing is trained — all adaptation lives in the prompt.

Key results

  • GSM8K, pre-trained PaLM 2-L scorer, A_begin: OPRO's best instruction "Take a deep breath and work on this problem step-by-step." scores 80.2 test accuracy vs 71.8 for human-written "Let's think step by step." (+8.4), 58.8 for APE's "Let's work this out in a step by step way to be sure we have the right answer.", 60.8 for "Let's solve the problem.", and 34.0 for the empty string. Other optimizers land just below: PaLM 2-L "Break this down." 79.9, gpt-3.5-turbo 78.5, gpt-4 74.5.
  • BBH, 23 tasks with a 20/80 train-test split, PaLM 2-L scorer: OPRO's found instructions beat "Let's think step by step." by over 5% on 19/23 tasks and beat the empty-string start on 20/23. Largest test-accuracy jumps over the CoT baseline: word_sorting 4.5→54.5 (+50.0, the source of the abstract's "up to 50%"), sports_understanding 46.5→88.0, movie_recommendation 52.5→90.5, object_counting 46.5→86.5, geometric_shapes 33.0→57.0.
  • BBH with the text-bison scorer, same pattern: >5% over the CoT baseline on 15/23 tasks, e.g. temporal_sequences 43.5→81.0 test, movie_recommendation 63.0→90.0, ruin_names 65.5→82.5, disambiguation_qa 6.0→73.0. Top GSM8K instructions also transfer within the math domain (Table 6): "Take a deep breath..." gives MultiArith 95.3 vs 85.7 for CoT and AQuA 54.3 vs 44.9.
  • TSP against a Gurobi oracle (5 instances per n): at n=10 every optimizer LLM reaches a 0.0% optimality gap while nearest-neighbor sits at 13.0% and farthest-insertion at 3.2%; gpt-4 needs 9.6±3.0 steps vs 40.4±5.6 (text-bison) and 46.8±9.3 (gpt-3.5-turbo), roughly 4x faster. At n=50 OPRO degrades: gpt-4 11.0%, gpt-3.5-turbo 133.0%, text-bison 219.8% gap, with farthest-insertion best at 9.8%.
  • Ablations (text-bison scorer, PaLM 2-L optimizer): 8 instructions per step beats 1/2/4/16 at equal evaluation budget; optimizer temperature 1.0 beats 0.0/0.5/1.5/2.0; ascending score order beats descending and random (recency bias); 3 exemplars beat 10 or none; and one-step generation of 50 instructions collapses (best of 50 on GSM8K was "Let's solve the problem" at 60.8 test, vs "Let's do the math!" at 76.3 test found within the first few OPRO steps).

Key advantages

  • Consumes the whole optimization trajectory — many score-sorted (solution, score) pairs — rather than editing a single prompt from natural-language feedback (APO) or forcing the new prompt to be semantically similar (APE); the LLM infers the direction of improvement itself, so accuracy keeps climbing across steps instead of saturating after one generation round.
  • Gradient-free, training-free and API-only, yet the identical meta-prompt template carries over from continuous optimization (linear regression coefficients) to combinatorial optimization (TSP tours) to prompt optimization — the task is retargeted by rewriting the natural-language description.
  • Decouples optimizer LLM from scorer LLM, and the gain holds across five models (PaLM 2-L, PaLM 2-L-IT, text-bison, gpt-3.5-turbo, gpt-4); pre-trained PaLM 2-L even improves its own predictions by prompting itself.
  • Each meta-prompt design choice is ablated rather than asserted (ordering, numeric scores, exemplar count, samples per step, temperature), and it beats EvoPrompt's GA/DE meta-prompts, which the paper shows actually degrade GSM8K because they carry no exemplars and depend on task-specific seed prompts.

Limitations

  • The paper states OPRO is not intended to beat gradient-based continuous optimizers or specialized combinatorial solvers: its TSP gap is worse than farthest-insertion at n=50 (11.0% vs 9.8%) and it fails on bumpy landscapes, getting stuck near (0,0) on the Rosenbrock function with a=20 instead of reaching (20,400).
  • Context-window bound: large problem descriptions (high-dimensional linear regression, TSP with many nodes) simply do not fit in the meta-prompt, so scale is capped by prompt length.
  • Prompt optimization requires a training set to compute the guiding accuracy (at least tens of samples), and the found instructions still overfit — Table 7 and Table 10 show training accuracies 5-20% above test, which the authors suggest mitigating via a larger training set and early stopping.
  • Appendix A failure modes across all optimizer LLMs: hallucinating objective values it should have computed, re-emitting solutions already present in context despite explicit instructions not to, and stalling when in-context exemplars share a wrong coefficient; using error cases instead of random exemplars gave similar results, so errors alone were not informative enough.

Where it fits the RSI arc

Stage 2, prompt/context optimization — the canonical trajectory-based, gradient-free optimizer and sibling of APE; Promptbreeder (2309.16797) self-referentially evolves OPRO's mutator. Pairs with TextGrad and DSPy in the "LLM optimizes the artifact" family, and its solution-score-pair loop recurs at harness level in stages 5-6.
