TextGrad: Automatic "Differentiation" via Text

Mert Yuksekgonul, Federico Bianchi, Joseph Boen, Sheng Liu, Zhi Huang, Carlos Guestrin, James Zou

Nature 2025 · 2025

Local PDFOnline

TL;DR

Backprop for compound AI systems: every text variable — prompt, code, answer, SMILES, dose weight — is a parameter, and an LLM emits natural-language criticism at each edge that is chained backward and applied by a textual-gradient-descent step.

Abstract

AI is undergoing a paradigm shift, with breakthroughs achieved by systems orchestrating multiple large language models (LLMs) and other complex components. As a result, developing principled and automated optimization methods for compound AI systems is one of the most important new challenges. Neural networks faced a similar challenge in its early days until backpropagation and automatic differentiation transformed the field by making optimization turn-key. Inspired by this, we introduce TextGrad, a powerful framework performing automatic ``differentiation'' via text. TextGrad backpropagates textual feedback provided by LLMs to improve individual components of a compound AI system. In our framework, LLMs provide rich, general, natural language suggestions to optimize variables in computation graphs, ranging from code snippets to molecular structures. TextGrad follows PyTorch's syntax and abstraction and is flexible and easy-to-use. It works out-of-the-box for a variety of tasks, where the users only provide the objective function without tuning components or prompts of the framework. We showcase TextGrad's effectiveness and generality across a diverse range of applications, from question answering and molecule optimization to radiotherapy treatment planning. Without modifying the framework, TextGrad improves the zero-shot accuracy of GPT-4o in Google-Proof Question Answering from $51\%$ to $55\%$, yields $20\%$ relative performance gain in optimizing LeetCode-Hard coding problem solutions, improves prompts for reasoning, designs new druglike small molecules with desirable in silico binding, and designs radiation oncology treatment plans with high specificity. TextGrad lays a foundation to accelerate the development of the next-generation of AI systems.

Method

Wraps a compound system as a PyTorch-like computation graph v = f(predecessors), where variables are arbitrary text (system prompt, code snippet, an answer, a molecule SMILES, a radiotherapy importance-weight string) and functions are LLM calls, simulators, or numerical optimizers. The forward pass records each variable's predecessors; the objective may be nondifferentiable — unit-test results, an Autodock Vina docking score, or an LLM judge. The backward pass walks the graph in reverse topological order: for each edge an LLM gradient operator receives the successor's gradient plus the forward conversation and returns criticism of the predecessor (context concatenated with feedback), and feedback from all successors is aggregated, mirroring autograd through addition — which also yields minibatching via tg.sum. TextualGradientDescent.step then rewrites the variable from its accumulated criticism. The API mirrors PyTorch (tg.Variable, BlackboxLLM, TextLoss, loss.backward(), optimizer.step()), at most one gradient call per edge per iteration.

Key results

  • LeetCode Hard with gpt-4o, 5 seeds (Table 1): completion rate 0.26 zero-shot and 0.31 ± 0.012 for Reflexion run one-shot → 0.36 ± 0.018 for TextGrad, which used zero demonstrations.
  • Zero-shot question answering (Table 2): GPQA 51.0 CoT / 53.6 best reported → 55.0; MMLU-Machine Learning 85.7 → 88.4; MMLU-College Physics 91.2 → 95.1.
  • Prompt optimization for gpt-3.5-turbo with gpt-4o as the gradient engine (Table 3): Object Counting 77.8 CoT and 84.9 for DSPy BFSR with 8 demonstrations → 91.9 instruction-only; GSM8k 72.9 / 81.1 → 81.1; Word Sorting 76.7 / 79.8 → 79.8.
  • Molecules over all 58 DOCKSTRING targets: one PPARA run moves Vina -4.2 kcal/mol, QED 0.44 → -5.5 / 0.59 → -7.5 / 0.79 (Fig. 2a); across the 29 targets with approved drugs TextGrad matches clinical Vina and exceeds clinical QED, at median Tversky similarity 0.42 to the approved drug (range 0.14–0.90).
  • Prostate radiotherapy, 5 patients (Supp. Tables 1–2): PTV D95 hits the 70.20 Gy prescription (+0.00 ± 0.00 vs oncologist -0.10 ± 0.15) and mean dose is +0.51 ± 0.09 Gy over goal vs +1.97 ± 0.36 for the oncologist; rectum mean dose 23.88 → 17.18 Gy, bladder 22.39 → 20.92 Gy.

Key advantages

  • One fixed framework: the gradient operator and the TGD prompt are identical across all five domains — only the objective function and the graph change — so a new application costs a loss function plus a few lines of PyTorch-shaped code.
  • Extends the textual-gradient idea from prompt optimization to instance optimization, which the paper names as its advance over DSPy and ProTeGi: the artifact itself (code, answer, molecule, hyperparameters) is the parameter, optimized at test time without a training set.
  • Textual gradients are legible, so the edit carries its rationale: Fig. 1e's gradient finds the nums[i] == k edge case that breaks the first gpt-4o solution, and Fig. 3's gradients walk from 'increase the PTV weight' to 'slightly increase the bladder and rectum weights' as the tradeoff moves.
  • Complementary to program search rather than competing: it matches DSPy BFSR on GSM8k (81.1) and Word Sorting (79.8) with instruction-only prompts and no demonstrations, beats it by 7 points on Object Counting, and DSPy's demonstrations plus the TextGrad prompt reach 82.1 on GSM8k — while keeping test-time inference cheaper.

Limitations

  • The authors' own §5 list: graph operations still do not cover tool use or retrieval-augmented components, and the molecule and radiotherapy results are in silico proof-of-concept — the ultimate test requires experimental and clinical assessment.
  • No convergence guarantee: variance reduction, adaptive gradients and LLM self-verification are named as future work, so nothing establishes that a textual-gradient step actually descends; gradients are unstructured text and can be noisy or uninformative.
  • Cost scales with the graph — up to one extra LLM call per edge per iteration plus the update call — and experiments are correspondingly small: 39 LeetCode problems, 12 prompt-optimization iterations at batch size 3, 10 molecule iterations per target.
  • Gradient quality rides on the critic model: prompt optimization uses gpt-4o to improve gpt-3.5-turbo, and the paper notes (citing prior work) that constraint-following reliability drops as natural-language constraints accumulate.

Where it fits the RSI arc

Stage 2 (Optimization-as-Language) anchor: the general-purpose optimizer that later harness-search loops such as DGM, RRSI and Harness-Zero loosely analogize — they evolve whole scaffolds, TextGrad differentiates any text variable inside one. Pairs with DSPy as its sibling (program and demonstration search), OPRO and Promptbreeder as prompt-only ancestors, and Reflexion as the single-variable special case.
