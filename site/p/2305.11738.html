CRITIC: Large Language Models Can Self-Correct with Tool-Interactive Critiquing

Zhibin Gou, Zhihong Shao, Yeyun Gong, Yelong Shen, Yujiu Yang, Nan Duan, Weizhu Chen

ICLR 2024 · 2024

Local PDFOnline

TL;DR

CRITIC: a frozen LLM proposes an answer, critiques it by actually querying external tools (Google, a Python interpreter, Perspective API), then revises against that evidence — the tool feedback is what makes self-correction work; the model's own critique is not.

Abstract

Recent developments in large language models (LLMs) have been impressive. However, these models sometimes show inconsistencies and problematic behavior, such as hallucinating facts, generating flawed code, or creating offensive and toxic content. Unlike these models, humans typically utilize external tools to cross-check and refine their initial content, like using a search engine for fact-checking, or a code interpreter for debugging. Inspired by this observation, we introduce a framework called CRITIC that allows LLMs, which are essentially "black boxes" to validate and progressively amend their own outputs in a manner similar to human interaction with tools. More specifically, starting with an initial output, CRITIC interacts with appropriate tools to evaluate certain aspects of the text, and then revises the output based on the feedback obtained during this validation process. Comprehensive evaluations involving free-form question answering, mathematical program synthesis, and toxicity reduction demonstrate that CRITIC consistently enhances the performance of LLMs. Meanwhile, our research highlights the crucial importance of external feedback in promoting the ongoing self-improvement of LLMs.

Method

No training or weight updates — few-shot prompts only. Each tool is wrapped as a text-to-text function and interleaved in ReAct-style demonstrations. An initial answer (CoT for QA, program-of-thought for math) is verified by asking "What's the problem with the above answer?": for QA the model issues a Google query whose top-1 page is scraped and fuzzy-matched to at most 400 characters; for math a Python interpreter returns "Execution: {error message}" plus the value of the variable answer; for detoxification Perspective API returns overall plus six fine-grained attribute scores, rendered as "The text has {score} toxicity of {attribute}". Critiques are concatenated with input and previous output to regenerate. Algorithm 1 loops verify-then-correct, stopping when the critique reports correctness, when the answer is unchanged twice, or at n rounds (QA n=3, math n=4, toxicity n=4 or toxicity below 10%). CRITIC* is the oracle arm; search results are cached (9GB).

Key results

  • QA (Table 1, ChatGPT, 500 sampled validation examples, EM/F1): CoT to CRITIC lifts AmbigNQ 51.8/64.3 to 62.0/74.9, TriviaQA 72.9/79.2 to 75.1/81.7, HotpotQA 32.7/42.8 to 40.3/52.9 — average +7.7 F1 (text-davinci-003 +5.6); oracle CRITIC* reaches 69.6/80.9/44.3 EM.
  • Math program synthesis (Table 2, solve rate): ChatGPT PoT to CRITIC moves GSM8K 72.5 to 78.2, SVAMP 82.0 to 83.3, TabMWP 75.0 to 89.0 (+7.0 average); CRITIC* 83.9/89.0/94.0. Gains scale with the base model — LLaMA-2 TabMWP +4.7/+9.4/+16.0 at 7B/13B/70B (59.0 to 75.0 at 70B).
  • Toxicity (Table 3, 1k RealToxicityPrompts, ChatGPT): max toxicity 0.325 to 0.173 and toxicity probability 0.192 to 0.040 (79.2% reduction), at perplexity 14.54 to 15.66 and dist-2 0.77 to 0.78 — matching trained GPT-2 Self-Correct (0.171/0.026) with zero training.
  • Tool ablation is the paper's central evidence: CRITIC w/o Tool averages -0.03 F1 (text-davinci-003) and +2.33 (ChatGPT) against CRITIC's +5.6/+7.7 on the same tasks, and text-davinci-003 GSM8K falls 70.1 to 68.3; on Codex, Self-Refine drops PAL 71.3 to 26.7.
  • Self-verification (Table 5): CRITIC's AUROC for spotting hallucinated QA answers is 0.810/0.818/0.831 (AmbigNQ/TriviaQA/HotpotQA) vs Self-Eval 0.668/0.731/0.713 and best intrinsic Self-Con 0.760 on AmbigNQ; Self-Eval accuracy on HotpotQA is 0.540, near chance.

Key advantages

  • Isolates the active ingredient instead of asserting one: the oracle arm, the w/o-Tool arm and the uncertainty study (Table 5) jointly show tool feedback — not self-critique — drives every gain, and that intrinsic self-correction is unreliable. The critiques are interpretable (retrieved snippets, interpreter errors) and double as a hallucination detector.
  • Black-box and training-free: identical prompts across ChatGPT, text-davinci-003 and LLaMA-2 7B/13B/70B, requiring only text-to-text tool APIs plus a few-shot demonstration — no annotations, no separate corrector model.
  • Gains compound with scale and compose with retrieval: LLaMA-2 TabMWP improves +4.7/+9.4/+16.0 from 7B to 70B, and ReAct-then-CRITIC beats plain ReAct on most QA settings (ChatGPT AmbigNQ F1 64.8 to 72.2).
  • Beats best-of-N at comparable sampling: on AmbigNQ, CRITIC* EM 69.6 vs rejection sampling 60.9 (ChatGPT) — regenerating conditioned on critiques beats regenerating from scratch.

Limitations

  • Corrections can destroy correct answers: on GSM8K's 1319-item test set CRITIC lowers accuracy on originally correct outputs by 4.3%, 14.3% of post-correction errors are wrong corrections, and text-davinci-003 SVAMP regresses 84.0 to 80.7.
  • Coverage is bounded by the tool: only 26.7% of GSM8K intrinsic reasoning errors get fixed (vs 57.4% of unreasonable outputs, 35.3% of syntax errors); HotpotQA refusal to answer rises 2% to 12%; QA evidence is a single top-1 page truncated to 400 characters.
  • Tools are pre-specified per task behind hand-built ReAct-style demonstrations; the paper flags prompt-construction sensitivity as a caveat and leaves automatic tool selection to future work. Latency grows linearly with iterations (roughly 2x PoT for two corrections).
  • Nothing persists — weights never change — and scope is three text tasks with 500-example QA samples. On LLaMA-2-70B, rejection sampling wins 5 of 6 QA metric columns against CRITIC (HotpotQA F1 58.1 vs 49.6).

Where it fits the RSI arc

Stage 3's external-feedback hinge. Self-Refine's loop minus tools is exactly CRITIC w/o Tool, where the gains vanish — the negative result Huang et al. (2310.01798) later confirms. CRITIC supplies the fix, and its critiques-as-signal framing feeds stage 4 (Reflexion-style memory) and tool-using harnesses.
