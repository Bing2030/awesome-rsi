AI-GAs: AI-Generating Algorithms, an Alternate Paradigm for Producing General Artificial Intelligence

Jeff Clune

arXiv 2019 · 2019

Local PDFOnline

TL;DR

Position paper: instead of hand-designing intelligence, build an AI-generating algorithm whose Three Pillars -- meta-learned architectures, meta-learned learning algorithms, and automatically generated learning environments -- are themselves searched for, so a simple outer loop bootstraps general AI.

Abstract

Perhaps the most ambitious scientific quest in human history is the creation of general artificial intelligence, which roughly means AI that is as smart or smarter than humans. The dominant approach in the machine learning community is to attempt to discover each of the pieces required for intelligence, with the implicit assumption that some future group will complete the Herculean task of figuring out how to combine all of those pieces into a complex thinking machine. I call this the "manual AI approach". This paper describes another exciting path that ultimately may be more successful at producing general AI. It is based on the clear trend in machine learning that hand-designed solutions eventually are replaced by more effective, learned solutions. The idea is to create an AI-generating algorithm (AI-GA), which automatically learns how to produce general AI. Three Pillars are essential for the approach: (1) meta-learning architectures, (2) meta-learning the learning algorithms themselves, and (3) generating effective learning environments. I argue that either approach could produce general AI first, and both are scientifically worthwhile irrespective of which is the fastest path. Because both are promising, yet the ML community is currently committed to the manual approach, I argue that our community should increase its research investment in the AI-GA approach. To encourage such research, I describe promising work in each of the Three Pillars. I also discuss AI-GA-specific safety and ethical considerations. Because it it may be the fastest path to general AI and because it is inherently scientifically interesting to understand the conditions in which a simple algorithm can produce general AI (as happened on Earth where Darwinian evolution produced human intelligence), I argue that the pursuit of AI-GAs should be considered a new grand challenge of computer science research.

Method

An AI-GA is an outer-loop search whose search space is learning machinery rather than a task, and whose outer optimizer may be meta-gradients, policy gradients, Bayesian optimization or evolution. Three Pillars: (1) meta-learn architectures; (2) meta-learn the learning algorithm -- MAML-style task distributions on fixed SGD, or an outer-loop-trained RNN whose activations implement the inner algorithm, with 'materials' such as Hebbian differentiable plasticity and neuromodulation as meta-learned hyperparameters; (3) generate the learning environments, data and curriculum. Pillar 3 has two modes. Target-task: a Generative Teaching Network, a recurrent (hence any-data-capable) DNN, emits data for a student net; both are differentiable, so target-task loss is backpropagated through the student's whole learning process into the GTN. Open-ended: POET co-optimizes environment parameters theta_E and agent weights theta_A, perturbing an environment whose agent passes a threshold, keeping only environments neither too hard for all nor too easy for any, with goal-switching between quality-diversity niches.

Key results

  • Generative Teaching Networks (Pillar 3, target-task): a GTN trained to produce data for a student learning MNIST emits images the paper calls 'completely unrecognizable and alien', yet the student learns to recognize real handwritten digits -- and 'learns four times faster than when training on real MNIST training data'.
  • ANML (Pillar 2, neuromodulation), reported as work done since v1: meta-learning a solution to catastrophic forgetting for deep networks at 'the unprecedented scale of 600 sequential tasks (over 9000 SGD updates)'.
  • The quality-diversity lineage the paper leans on for Pillar 3 posts real numbers on hard problems: a Nature paper using QD achieves state-of-the-art robot damage recovery, adapting to substantial damage in 1-2 minutes; Go-Explore (MAP-Elites plus a deterministic simulator) solves Montezuma's Revenge and Pitfall, 'dramatically improving the state of the art'; Curiosity Search matched the then state of the art on Montezuma's Revenge.
  • Pillar 2's other demonstrations are stated qualitatively here: MAML-style meta-learning is claimed to theoretically encode any learning algorithm, outer-loop RNNs learn to explore, exploit and balance on simple RL tasks and pass admittedly imperfect tests distinguishing model-based from model-free control, and differentiable-plasticity Hebbian RNNs outperform LSTMs on problems requiring remembering lots of information over time.
  • The investment case is quantified only as a decision threshold: even if one believes the AI-GA path has a 1%-5% chance of being the first to produce general AI, 'we should allocate corresponding resources into the field to catalyze its progress'.

Key advantages

  • Names the missing pillar rather than restating the obvious: Pillars 1 and 2 have decades of work, but 'little has been done on the third' -- generating effective learning environments and curricula -- and 'no work to date on attempting to combine the Three Pillars'. This is the clearest statement in the literature that environment generation, not model or optimizer design, is the open frontier.
  • Makes the self-improvement bet falsifiable instead of mystical: the outer loop is explicitly not restricted to evolution (meta-gradients, policy gradients, Bayesian optimization and hybrid evolutionary/policy-gradient methods all qualify), and the target-task GTN is a working end-to-end instantiation -- differentiable environment generation backpropagated through the student's learning -- not just a manifesto.
  • Gives Pillar 3 an explicit reward-function proposal for the open-ended case: environments count as useful when transferring agents (1) perform well faster than if trained from scratch and (2) still exhibit learning progress (neither too easy nor too hard), which together force shared structure across environments and block arbitrarily different, uninteresting challenges.
  • Supplies the existence proof and the diversity argument: Darwinian evolution is 'the first general-intelligence-generating algorithm', so the concept is known to work, and AI-GAs would yield a diversity of intelligences to reverse-engineer (a form of cultural travel) while catalyzing -- not replacing -- the manual path, which the paper argues should also be pursued.

Limitations

  • Compute is self-acknowledged as the central risk: AI-GAs 'will require extraordinary amounts of computation by today's standards', and the paper concedes the manual path is currently more compute-efficient, so the AI-GA path may simply lose the race; whether abstraction can shave orders of magnitude off evolution's cost is a prediction, not a result.
  • The pivotal question is left unanswered: for the open-ended version the author writes 'I do not have an answer to what this environment-generator reward function should be', and the proposal he does offer 'needs to be developed, improved, and experimentally investigated'.
  • Expressiveness is an unresolved dilemma: POET's encoding only produces obstacle courses (no doors, chess, swimming worlds, sound or smell), while GTNs 'may prove too expressive and thus make searching their vast search space intractable'; the paper floats constraining generators to physics simulators, and its other reward ideas are withheld as work in progress.
  • It is a position paper with no experiments of its own: every number cited comes from prior work by the author's group (GTN, POET, ANML, differentiable plasticity), and the paper's own safety analysis counts against the approach -- AI-GAs are 'by definition, attempting to create a runaway process', likely to yield values less aligned with ours and an inscrutable black box, and may create untold suffering in virtual agents.
