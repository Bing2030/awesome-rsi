Frontis-MA1: Training an AI4AI Model towards Recursive Self-Improvement in Machine Learning Engineering

Junlin Yang, Che Jiang, Yu Fu, Tianwei Luo, Can Ren, Weizhi Wang, Kaikai Zhao, Hongyi Liu, Yuxin Zuo, Yuru Wang, Yuchen Fan, Kai Tian, Zhenzhao Yuan, Xiaojian Lin, Li Sheng, Rushi Qiang, Guoli Jia, Xingtai Lv, Ermo Hua, Dianqiao Lei, Youbang Sun, Ning Ding, Bowen Zhou, Kaiyan Zhang

arXiv 2026 · 2026

Local PDFOnline

TL;DR

Frontis/OpenMLE: an open three-part stack (gym, operator RL, evolutionary search) that post-trains a 35B model as a meta-evolution agent, lifting MLE-Bench Lite Medal Average from 39.39% to 60.61%, and 71.21% with Evo-Max.

Abstract

Recursive self-improvement (RSI) requires AI systems that improve the process of building AI (i.e., AI4AI); machine learning engineering (MLE) offers a concrete, executable testbed for studying this capability. We introduce OpenMLE, an open full-stack system for RSI research in MLE, spanning verifiable task environments with execution feedback (OpenMLE-Gym), operator learning (OpenMLE-RL), and long-horizon search (OpenMLE-Evo). On this stack we post-train Frontis-MA1 (35B) as a meta-evolution agent for MLE, aligning post-training and inference around four atomic program-evolution operators (Draft, Improve, Debug, Crossover): the same operators are trained via execution-grounded SFT and RL on data deduplicated against all evaluation benchmarks, then composed into long-horizon search, coupling learning and evolution in a single loop. On MLE-Bench Lite under a 12-hour per-task budget on one RTX 4090 capped at 12 GB VRAM, Frontis-MA1 (35B) improves Medal Average from 39.39% to 60.61% over its base model with OpenMLE-Evo, and reaches 71.21% with OpenMLE-Evo-Max (benchmark-independent experience priors and asynchronous search), exceeding GPT-5.5 + Codex and approaching GPT-5.6 Sol and the 2.8T Kimi K3. On held-out NatureBench Lite, both components transfer: with the framework fixed, swapping in the trained model raises Match-SOTA from 50% to 70%; with the model fixed, swapping in OpenMLE-Evo raises it from 20% to 50%. We release the model weights and the full OpenMLE stack to enable reproducible research on executable AI4AI toward RSI. Code: https://github.com/FrontisAI/OpenRSI

Method

OpenMLE is a three-layer stack. Gym packages 5,758 quality-gated executable tasks (156 curated anchors, 3,362 Kaggle-dataset tasks, 2,240 competitions) into one contract: public data, hidden evaluator returning a scalar score, sandboxed execution, structured diagnostics. Four atomic operators (Draft, Improve, Debug, Crossover) are the shared interface between training and inference. ERL post-trains the base model (Qwen3.6-35B-A3B) in two stages: budget-adaptive SFT on a 26,259-example corpus (17,245 Draft responses distilled from GLM-4.7/Qwen3 teachers, 9,014 trajectory steps harvested from AIRA-Evo search trees), then GSPO RL with adaptive per-task reward bounds, an entropic advantage concentrating signal on each rollout group's upper tail, asynchronous rollouts, and parent selection by reward, child-reward variance and visit cooling. Evo composes the same trained operators at test time over structured experience cards, three-factor parent selection (quality, improvement, novelty) and on-demand operator-conditioned memory.

Key results

  • MLE-Bench Lite (22 tasks, 12 h per task on one RTX 4090 capped at 12 GB VRAM), identical OpenMLE-Evo harness: post-training lifts Frontis-MA1-35B over its Qwen3.6-35B-A3B base from 39.39% to 60.61% Medal Average (Human Rank 0.5828 -> 0.7647, Valid Rate 19.67/22 -> 21.67/22, +21.22 pp); the companion 30B reproduces it, 34.85% -> 53.03% over Qwen3-30B-A3B-Thinking-2507 (three-epoch means: 39.39% +/- 5.67% -> 60.61% +/- 7.73%).
  • With OpenMLE-Evo-Max (MLE-Bench-disjoint cross-task priors plus asynchronous parallel search, same sandbox budget) the 35B system reaches 71.21% / 0.8126 Human Rank / 22.00-of-22 valid, above GPT-5.5 + Codex (63.64% in Table 1; 68.18% final test in Fig. 12) and just below GPT-5.6 Sol and Kimi K3 (72.73% each); the 30B reaches 66.67% / 0.8053.
  • Harness gain at fixed model: against original AIRA-Evo on the same Frontis-MA1-35B, OpenMLE-Evo raises Medal Average 53.03% -> 60.61%, and against general coding scaffolds it moves GLM-5.2 59.09% -> 62.12%, MiniMax M3 54.55% -> 59.09%, Kimi K2.6 59.09% -> 66.67% and MiniMax M2.7 45.50% -> 50.00% (Claude Code / Codex references).
  • The gains arrive late in the horizon and the search is cheaper: Improve and Crossover account for 85.0% of the total validation gain on leaf-classification and 91.9% on the audio task (held-out Human Rank 0.9455 Bronze and 0.8889 Silver, vs best comparisons 0.732 / 0.2963 with no medal); versus original AIRA-Evo at the same checkpoint, seed and 12 h, total model tokens fall 129.3M -> 75.3M (-41.7%) and Improve prompt mean 102.8K -> 35.7K characters (-65.3%) while evaluated nodes drop only 3430 -> 3004 (-12.4%), so new-best validation updates per million tokens rise 1.77 -> 3.27 (+84.3%) and Improve calls setting a new best go 4.73% -> 9.36%.
  • Held-out transfer on the 10-task NatureBench Lite subset (4 h per task): with the adapter fixed, swapping in the trained model raises Match-SOTA 50% -> 70% (5/10 -> 7/10) and Surpass-SOTA 20% -> 30% (2/10 -> 3/10); with the base model fixed, swapping in the OpenMLE-Evo adapter raises Match-SOTA 20% -> 50% (2/10 -> 5/10) and Surpass-SOTA 10% -> 20%.

Key advantages

  • The two gains are measured independently and compose, which is what makes the meta-evolution claim testable: at fixed harness, post-training buys +21.22 pp (39.39% -> 60.61%); at fixed model, Evo-Max buys +10.60 pp (60.61% -> 71.21%). Both are controlled replications (35B and 30B backbones), and the modality stratification attributes the 14 extra medals across every group (+2/+4/+1/+4/+3 image/text/tabular/audio/multimodal) with no group-level Medal Rate lowered.
  • Open-weight post-training, not scaffolding: under one common harness the post-trained 35B-A3B model (60.61%) beats MiniMax M3 (59.09%), Doubao Seed 2.1 Pro (56.06%) and DeepSeek-V4-Pro (54.55%), and with Evo-Max (71.21%) exceeds GLM-5.2 and MiniMax M3 under the same enhanced harness.
  • The harness itself is the contribution and is domain-specialised rather than model-specific: at fixed model, OpenMLE-Evo beats original AIRA-Evo (53.03% -> 60.61%) and beats Claude Code / Codex scaffolds on four frontier models, so the gain is not a single favourable model-harness pairing.
  • Cheap in both senses: 12 hours per task on a single RTX 4090 capped at 12 GB VRAM, a smaller sandbox budget than most reported MLE-Bench runs; and the bounded, operator-conditioned context cuts total model tokens 41.7% and prompt tokens 50.3% while raising discoveries per token 84.3%, so the mechanism improves search productivity rather than merely truncating it.

Limitations

  • Not RSI yet, per the paper's own section 8 list of five boundaries: training learns only from executed outcome, not whether a direction is promising, generalisable or robust; the trained operators are composed by an external harness rather than a general coding agent; the agent improves external ML artifacts but not language models themselves; evolution operates over candidate solutions while the evolutionary system stays fixed; and parent selection uses only three of the recorded experience signals.
  • Breadth is thin where it matters: the headline is one 22-task MLE-Bench Lite split and the transfer study is a fixed 10-task NatureBench Lite subset, where the reported gains are one or two tasks (7/10 vs 5/10; 2/10 vs 5/10). The authors state that small modality groups and the ten-task transfer set limit both conclusions.
  • The compute comparison is deliberately unnormalised: footnote 3 of section 6.1 says it uses per-run accelerator allocation and wall-clock budget from the official MLE-Bench runs registry and explicitly does not compare model-inference cost or normalise different accelerators to FLOPs.
  • Reproducibility is partial and the release is described inconsistently: licensing limits full task-package data to 1,415 of 5,758 tasks (the rest ship only prepare.py and metric.py), the SFT corpus is distilled from proprietary teachers with DeepSeek-V4-Pro as the trajectory annotator, Evo-Max's gain partly rests on priors distilled from public competition artifacts, and section 1 promises the release in the future tense while the abstract states it as done.

Where it fits the RSI arc

Stage 8 (Automated AI R&D), at the meta-evolution rung: it turns test-time MLE search into weights and back, one level above AIRA-Evo-style fixed-model harnesses and one below agents that improve the model itself. Pairs with MLE-Bench, MLE-Dojo, MLEvolve and FT-Dojo (2606.06473, 2603.01712), OpenEvolve/DGM, and stage 5's harness-evolution cards.
