Self-Consistency Improves Chain of Thought Reasoning in Language Models

Xuezhi Wang, Jason Wei, Dale Schuurmans, Quoc Le, Ed Chi, Sharan Narang, Aakanksha Chowdhery, Denny Zhou

ICLR 2023 · 2023

Local PDFOnline

TL;DR

Sampling many diverse chain-of-thought paths and taking a majority vote over their final answers lifts accuracy by roughly 3-24 points over greedy CoT, with no training, no verifier and no extra data.

Abstract

Chain-of-thought prompting combined with pre-trained large language models has achieved encouraging results on complex reasoning tasks. In this paper, we propose a new decoding strategy, self-consistency, to replace the naive greedy decoding used in chain-of-thought prompting. It first samples a diverse set of reasoning paths instead of only taking the greedy one, and then selects the most consistent answer by marginalizing out the sampled reasoning paths. Self-consistency leverages the intuition that a complex reasoning problem typically admits multiple different ways of thinking leading to its unique correct answer. Our extensive empirical evaluation shows that self-consistency boosts the performance of chain-of-thought prompting with a striking margin on a range of popular arithmetic and commonsense reasoning benchmarks, including GSM8K (+17.9%), SVAMP (+11.0%), AQuA (+12.2%), StrategyQA (+6.4%) and ARC-challenge (+3.9%).

Method

Prompt the LM with the same few-shot chain-of-thought exemplars as Wei et al. (8 for arithmetic, 4-7 for commonsense), then replace greedy decoding with "sample-and-marginalize". Sample m reasoning paths (r_i, a_i) independently - T=0.5 with top-k=40 for UL2-20B and LaMDA-137B, T=0.7, k=40 for PaLM-540B, T=0.7 with no top-k for GPT-3 - and parse each final answer a_i after "The answer is". Marginalize out the latent paths by majority vote, argmax_a sum_i 1(a_i=a); main results average 10 runs of 40 paths. No training, verifier, re-ranker or annotation - a "self-ensemble" over a single model. Aggregation was ablated: unweighted majority vote (74.4 GSM8K) matches length-normalized probability weighting (74.1), because normalized path probabilities are too close to discriminate; unnormalized weighting collapses to 59.9.

Key results

  • PaLM-540B: GSM8K 56.5 -> 74.4 (+17.9), AQuA 35.8 -> 48.3 (+12.5), SVAMP 79.0 -> 86.6 (+7.6), MultiArith 94.7 -> 99.3 (+4.6); commonsense StrategyQA 75.3 -> 81.6 (+6.3) and ARC-c 85.2 -> 88.7 (+3.5), over greedy CoT.
  • GPT-3 code-davinci-002: GSM8K 60.1 -> 78.0 (+17.9), SVAMP 75.8 -> 86.8 (+11.0), AQuA 39.8 -> 52.0 (+12.2), MultiArith 96.2 -> 100.0, CSQA 79.0 -> 81.5 - new SoTA without any supervised fine-tuning.
  • Gains scale with model size: UL2-20B only +3.2 to +6.8 points (GSM8K 4.1 -> 7.3) vs LaMDA-137B +9.1 to +23.9 (MultiArith 51.8 -> 75.7, GSM8K 17.1 -> 27.7), and GPT-3 code-davinci-001 +6.7 to +23.2.
  • Beats other uses of the same compute at matched budget: vs beam search on UL2-20B AQuA (beam-40 = 10.2, beam-1 = 23.6 vs self-consistency 26.9 +/- 0.5 over 40 sampled paths); vs prompt ensembles on LaMDA-137B GSM8K (3 prompt sets 18.6, 40 prompt permutations 19.2 vs 27.7); vs multi-model ensembles (LaMDA-137B + PaLM-540B = 36.9 vs 74.4 for PaLM-540B alone).
  • Robust and re-usable elsewhere: holds across three prompt sets (PaLM GSM8K 74.4 / 72.1 / 70.4), across sampling parameters (T, top-k, nucleus p), on imperfect CoT prompts (LaMDA-137B GSM8K 14.9 -> 23.4, above the 17.1 of a correct prompt with greedy decode), equation-only rationales, and zero-shot CoT (PaLM 43.0 -> 69.2).

Key advantages

  • Establishes inference-time diversity plus answer agreement as a training-free accuracy lever: new SoTA on GSM8K, SVAMP, AQuA and StrategyQA with no additional training, annotation or auxiliary model - beating prior work that required fine-tuning on 7.5k examples plus a second 175B verifier (GSM8K 55 vs 74.4).
  • Shows the gain comes from diversity of reasoning paths, not merely from more samples: it dominates beam search (which degrades as beams grow, 23.6 -> 10.2 AQuA), sample-and-rank, prompt-order and multi-prompt ensembles, and cross-model ensembles, at equal sample count.
  • Turns agreement into a usable uncertainty signal: the share of decodes agreeing with the majority answer correlates strongly with accuracy, giving a calibration / "know when it doesn't know" estimate for free.
  • The primitive is portable and cheap: compatible with any sampling scheme, with zero-shot CoT and with non-natural-language rationales (equations); majority-vote-over-self-samples is the mechanism later RSI pipelines inherit for aggregating debate (stage 6) and for filtering self-generated data.

Limitations

  • Cost multiplies with the number of paths (40 in the main results) for one final answer; the paper's own advice is to start with 5-10 paths, since the accuracy curve saturates quickly.
  • It only applies where the final answer comes from a fixed answer set that can be parsed and counted; open-ended generation needs an externally defined consistency metric.
  • The model cannot distinguish good reasoning from bad: normalized path probabilities cluster closely enough that probability weighting adds nothing over vote counting, and the paper notes models still emit incorrect or nonsensical rationales (the StrategyQA population figures in Table 4), leaving rationale grounding to future work.
  • No correctness guarantee - agreement is only a proxy, and the premise that correct reasoning is more consistent than incorrect reasoning is argued by intuition rather than proved, so a confidently wrong majority survives the vote.

Where it fits the RSI arc

Stage 3's founding inference-time result and the baseline the stage's papers argue against: nothing is revised, reliability comes from aggregating diverse samples of one frozen model. Debate aggregation (stage 6) and consistency filtering of self-generated data inherit the primitive. Pairs with Self-Refine; the negative result targets revision, not voting.
