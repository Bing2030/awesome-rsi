Quality Diversity: A New Frontier for Evolutionary Computation

Justin K. Pugh, Lisa B. Soros, Kenneth O. Stanley

Frontiers in Robotics and AI 2016 · 2016

Local PDFOnline

TL;DR

Quality-diversity algorithms return an archive of diverse, high-quality behaviors from one run; on deceptive gauntlet mazes the standard recipe (diversity driven by an unaligned behavior characterization) collapses, and driving search with aligned plus unaligned BCs at once recovers it.

Method

Position piece plus original experiments. It separates pure optimization (generational NEAT, population 500) from pure divergence (novelty search, k=20 neighbors, archive 2,500) and defines quality diversity (QD) as their intersection: pressure to discover behavioral niches plus pressure to raise quality within each. The analytic tool is BC alignment - whether novelty tends to raise fitness, judged by each behavior mapping to a narrow fitness range and neighboring behaviors having correlated fitness. Fifteen treatments run on wheeled-robot maze navigation: Fitness and NS controls; NSLC (local competition over k=20 behavioral neighbors) and MAP-Elites (1,024 behavior bins, one elite each) with MENOV and MEPGD variants; and four new multi-BC hybrids driven by EndpointBC (2-D final position, aligned) and DirectionBC (5-D facing direction, unaligned) simultaneously, with DirectionBC always measuring QD-score.

Key results

  • 900 runs in total: 15 treatments x 20 runs on three mazes (small 900x770, asymmetric 6000x6000, symmetric 4600x5280); small-maze runs ended at 250,000 evaluations, both gauntlets at 1,000,000.
  • On the symmetric gauntlet every DirectionBC-driven treatment solved significantly less often than plain Fitness (p < 0.05), and only MEPGDd found any solution at all - two solutions across all 20 runs - while every approach including EndpointBC beat Fitness (p < 0.001). QD need not cost optimization: NSLCe is competitive with the established deception-beating baseline NSe and may exceed it on the symmetric gauntlet, though not significantly (p = 0.093).
  • QD-score (sum of best fitness per DirectionBC bin; 1,024 bins, maximum 1,024,000 on the small maze) is dominated by the multi-BC hybrids: NSeNSLCd is among the best on all three mazes, unmatched on the asymmetric gauntlet and tied with NSLCe on the symmetric one (p = 0.163).
  • On the easy small maze the ordering inverts - the best treatments approach the 1,024,000 maximum and DirectionBC-driven multi-BC treatments (MEeMEd, MENOVeMENOVd, NSeNSLCd) plus NSLCd lead, while EndpointBC-driven treatments, NSd and Fitness trail.
  • MEd is unstable: most runs score 950K-1000K but three outliers score 791K, 680K and 421K, each with a completely filled grid holding low-quality agents that circle inside the main deceptive trap.

Key advantages

  • Formalizes quality diversity as a distinct research program and supplies the design rule that makes it work: BC alignment, predictable from whether each behavior maps to a narrow fitness range and whether neighboring behaviors have correlated fitness.
  • Shows empirically that the field's default - driving QD with the unaligned BC you want diversity in - degrades monotonically with maze difficulty and fails outright on the hardest maze, so QD does not escape deception by itself.
  • Introduces multi-BC hybrids (notably NS-NSLC: aligned novelty, unaligned novelty and unaligned local competition Pareto-ranked via NSGA-II) and demonstrates they win QD-score across all three mazes.
  • Reconciles QD with optimization: the objective-less search for behavioral diversity can be rectified with the missing objective without reintroducing deception, with practical diagnostics (run pure fitness to test difficulty, run NS with the candidate aligned BC to test alignment).

Limitations

  • Statistical significance uses unpaired two-tailed Student's t-tests deliberately unadjusted for multiple comparisons across 15 treatments, so results are meant to flag candidate differences rather than settle a ranking.
  • The domain is simulated wheeled-robot maze navigation with a single continuous turn output and no physics cost - a cheap proxy; strict gauntlet time limits also make some behavior bins unsolvable, so the gauntlet QD-score ceiling is unreachable in principle.
  • Alignment cannot be measured a priori; the paper offers only heuristic probes for deciding whether a domain is hard enough to need multiple BCs and whether a chosen aligned BC is sufficiently aligned.
  • Coverage is partial: MAP-Elites is described as a largely unexplored paradigm, and concatenating BCs into one higher-dimensional BC, MEPGDNOV and MEPGD-MEPGD are proposed but left untested.

Where it fits the RSI arc
