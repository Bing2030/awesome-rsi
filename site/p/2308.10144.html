ExpeL: LLM Agents Are Experiential Learners

Andrew Zhao, Daniel Huang, Quentin Xu, Matthieu Lin, Yong-Jin Liu, Gao Huang

AAAI 2024 · 2024

Local PDFOnline

TL;DR

ExpeL learns without gradients: it retries training tasks via Reflexion, distills same-task fail/success pairs and success batches into an editable natural-language insight list, and at test time concatenates those insights plus the k most task-similar past successes to a ReAct agent.

Abstract

The recent surge in research interest in applying large language models (LLMs) to decision-making tasks has flourished by leveraging the extensive world knowledge embedded in LLMs. While there is a growing demand to tailor LLMs for custom decision-making tasks, finetuning them for specific tasks is resource-intensive and may diminish the model's generalization capabilities. Moreover, state-of-the-art language models like GPT-4 and Claude are primarily accessible through API calls, with their parametric weights remaining proprietary and unavailable to the public. This scenario emphasizes the growing need for new methodologies that allow learning from agent experiences without requiring parametric updates. To address these problems, we introduce the Experiential Learning (ExpeL) agent. Our agent autonomously gathers experiences and extracts knowledge using natural language from a collection of training tasks. At inference, the agent recalls its extracted insights and past experiences to make informed decisions. Our empirical results highlight the robust learning efficacy of the ExpeL agent, indicating a consistent enhancement in its performance as it accumulates experiences. We further explore the emerging capabilities and transfer learning potential of the ExpeL agent through qualitative observations and additional experiments.

Method

Three stages over a ReAct base, zero weight updates. (1) Gather: each training task is retried at most Z=3 times via Reflexion; every trajectory enters experience pool B (Faiss kNN + all-mpnet-base-v2, seeded with the manual fewshots) and each failure emits a self-reflection concatenated into the next attempt, so B holds successes and same-task fail/success pairs. (2) Extract: gpt-4-0613 (LLM_insights) is fed either a fail/success pair or a chunk of L successes (L=8 HotpotQA/ALFWorld, 4 WebShop) sampled without replacement, and rewrites the growing insight list through ADD / EDIT / UPVOTE / DOWNVOTE; a new insight starts at importance count 2, gains 1 on upvote or edit, loses 1 on downvote, and is deleted at 0. (3) Infer: one attempt per unseen task, task spec augmented with all of insights plus top-k task-similar successful trajectories (k=6 HotpotQA, 2 ALFWorld/WebShop).

Key results

  • HotpotQA (Table 3): ReAct 28.0 ± 1.4 → ExpeL 39.0 ± 1.7; ablations 32.0 ± 1.1 (human-crafted insights), 32.0 ± 0.4 (gpt-3.5-turbo as insight LLM), 29.0 ± 0.4 (reflections added to insight extraction).
  • ALFWorld (Table 3, retrieval ablation): ReAct 40.0 ± 0.3 → ExpeL 59.0 ± 0.3, with reason-similarity retrieval 48.5 ± 2.1 and random retrieval 42.5 ± 0.8. Per-category (Table 5), ExpeL vs ReAct: put 83 vs 50, look 39 vs 0, heat 43 vs 13.
  • WebShop (Table 5): mean environment reward ExpeL 0.701 vs ReAct 0.665 and Act 0.666; single-mode variants sit at 0.675 (insights-only) and 0.67 (retrieve-only), whose success rates are 37% / 38%.
  • The two learning modes are complementary and domain-specific: restricting to one gives HotpotQA 36% / 31% (insights / retrieve) and ALFWorld 50% / 55%, and an agent extracting insights from fewshots alone shows no gain over ReAct.
  • Transfer HotpotQA → FEVER (Table 1): Act 58 ± 0.0, ReAct 63 ± 0.4, ExpeL Transfer without task demos 65 ± 1.7, ExpeL Transfer 70 ± 0.7. Adding Reflexion retries on ALFWorld (Table 2): ExpeL 59.0 → 64.2 across R0–R3 vs ReAct+Reflexion 40.3 → 54.4.

Key advantages

  • Inter-task learning that pays off without retries at deployment: ExpeL's single-attempt ALFWorld 59.0 beats Reflexion's R3 54.4, so it improves where reattempts are unavailable, and it never needs model weights, making it usable with closed API models (GPT-4/Claude).
  • The ablations show each stage is load-bearing and not interchangeable: insights dominate HotpotQA (36 vs 31) while retrieved trajectories dominate ALFWorld (55 vs 50), and task-similarity ranking clearly beats reason-similarity (48.5) and random (42.5) retrieval.
  • Every artifact stays in natural language - insights and trajectories are inspectable, editable, deletable, and expert insights can be inserted - and autonomously learned insights beat hand-crafted ones (39.0 vs 32.0), with a stronger insight LLM lifting the ceiling (gpt-4 vs gpt-3.5: 39.0 vs 32.0).
  • Knowledge transfers across distributions with only a few target demonstrations: adapting HotpotQA insights with FEVER fewshots raises 63 ± 0.4 to 70 ± 0.7 without any target-task experience pool.

Limitations

  • Text-only observations, by the authors' own account: image observations (via VLMs or captioning models) are left to future work, so the results do not cover real-world embodied settings.
  • All experiments use closed-source API LLMs (gpt-3.5-turbo-0613 for policy/reflection, gpt-4-0613 for insights); open-source LLMs are named as untested future work, which the authors flag as off-limits in some applications.
  • The insight list is assumed to fit the context window; truly lifelong agents would need a retrieval step over insights to bound context, which is proposed but not implemented or evaluated.
  • Prompting lacks the theoretical grounding of RL (no optimality guarantee), and the paper concedes WebShop stays on the lower side of Reflexion's success range; reflections added to insight extraction hurt (29.0 vs 39.0) because they hallucinate.
