PAPER · stage 6 · Multi-Agent Self-Improvement

DEBATE, TRAIN, EVOLVE: Self Evolution of Language Model Reasoning

Gaurav Srivastava, Zhenyu Bi, Meng Lu, Xuan Wang

EMNLP 2025 · 2025

Local PDFOnline

TL;DR

No-label self-evolution: run RCR-prompted multi-agent debate, keep the consensus answer plus a consolidated rationale, GRPO-train the single model on them, then swap the evolved policy back into the debate team and repeat. +8.92 pts average on GSM-Plus after one round.

Abstract

Large language models (LLMs) have improved significantly in their reasoning through extensive training on massive datasets. However, relying solely on additional data for improvement is becoming increasingly impractical, highlighting the need for models to autonomously enhance their reasoning without external supervision. In this paper, we propose Debate, Train, Evolve (DTE), a novel ground truth-free training framework that uses multi-agent debate traces to evolve a single language model. We also introduce a new prompting strategy Reflect-Critique-Refine, to improve debate quality by explicitly instructing agents to critique and refine their reasoning. Extensive evaluations on seven reasoning benchmarks with six open-weight models show that our DTE framework achieve substantial improvements, with an average accuracy gain of 8.92% on the challenging GSM-PLUS dataset. Furthermore, we observe strong cross-domain generalization, with an average accuracy gain of 5.8% on all other benchmarks, suggesting that our method captures general reasoning capabilities. Our framework code and trained models are publicly available at https://github.com/ctrl-gaurav/Debate-Train-Evolve

Method

Agents A={a1..aN} debate a query for up to T rounds under the RCR prompt, which enforces three phases per round: Reflect (self-critique of own rationale), Critique (exactly two peers), Refine (update the answer, and any changed answer must add a novel reasoning step absent from all prior traces). Debate stops on unanimous consensus, else majority vote. Traces are compressed into one consolidated rationale R by keeping steps that recur across agents or introduce novel symbolic manipulation, yielding (q, y*, R). Training is GRPO on that instance with reward r = 2.0·1[y=y*] + 0.5·f_format(y) + 0.5·exp(−|y|/120) — the correctness term is agreement with the debate consensus, not ground truth. G=8 samples/query, clip 0.2, KL β=0.02, LoRA r=128 on attention/MLP projections, AdamW lr 5e-6. After each round the evolved policy replaces its own predecessor in the debate pool.

Key results

  • One DTE round (Table 1, GSM-Plus): Qwen-1.5B 42.00 → 55.92 (+13.92), Qwen-3B 61.75 → 69.50, Qwen-7B 68.62 → 74.71, Qwen-14B 71.79 → 78.88, Llama-3B 45.67 → 53.79, Llama-8B 55.62 → 66.17 — mean +8.92 pts over the base model at one forward pass. GSM8K gains average only +0.84 (models near ceiling) and ARC-Challenge is mixed: +3.67 Qwen-7B, +8.88 Llama-8B, but −0.85 Qwen-1.5B and −0.42 Qwen-3B. Across all five Table 1 benchmarks the evolved single model averages +3.06 pts over the base model and +1.09 pts over the 3-agent RCR committee that generated its traces.
  • Cross-domain transfer (Table 2): evolving on GSM8K gives +5.8 pts average on the unseen GSM-Plus, +2.5 on ARC-Challenge, and CommonsenseQA gains for 5/6 models; models ≥7B never lose more than 0.2 pts on any transfer task, while Qwen-1.5B drops 1.60 on ARC-Easy and 2.23 on CommonsenseQA. Evolving on GSM-Plus and testing on GSM8K is symmetric (+3.7 avg), which the authors read as learning heuristics (numeric decomposition, unit tracking) rather than dataset patterns.
  • The RCR prompt alone (ablation 1, eight models) beats the original MAD@3 prompt by +1.9 pts GSM8K, +3.7 GSM-Plus, +0.7 ARC-Challenge — up to +7.9 (Qwen-1.5B) and +6.1 (Qwen-7B) on GSM-Plus. It halves the mean sycophancy rate (0.28 → 0.13 on GSM-Plus) and narrows the verbosity gap by 43%, versus standard MAD where small-model sycophancy exceeds 28% on average.
  • GRPO beats both alternatives at fixed compute (Table 3, GSM-Plus, 10K steps): Qwen-1.5B 42.00 → SFT 47.31 / DPO 51.34 / GRPO 55.92; Qwen-3B 61.75 → SFT 58.33 (regression) / DPO 64.32 / GRPO 69.50; Qwen-7B 68.62 → 67.89 / 69.88 / 74.71. GRPO holds KL < 0.24 across sizes where DPO averages 0.43.
  • Effort scaling: 3 agents capture 85–95% of the achievable debate gain (ARC-Easy saturates at 2, GSM-Plus sometimes prefers 4–5; Qwen-7B peaks at 76.0% with 7 agents, +1.04 over 3). GRPO saturates near 8K steps (+0.32 pts average from 8K → 10K while wall-clock grows 25%). A second evolution round gives ≥7B at most +0.8 pts and often loses 0.4–1.4 (mean forgetting Fgt2 = 0.92 pts ≥7B, 1.6 smaller). Trace coverage is monotonic (Table 4): All-Traces beats Debate-Only by 4.43 pts and Random-2K by 9.17.

Key advantages

  • Self-referential where fixed-judge self-training is not: after each round the evolved policy replaces its own predecessor inside the debate ensemble (Algorithm 2, step 18), so trace generator and trace consumer improve together — and the evolved single model then beats the very committee (+1.09 pts on average across Table 1's five benchmarks) that produced its training data.
  • RCR is diagnosed before it is proposed: the paper first measures standard MAD's failure modes (sycophancy above 28% average for small models, verbosity bias, [correct → incorrect] transitions) and then fixes them mechanically — a fixed two-peer critique quota and a mandatory novel reasoning step on any answer change — halving sycophancy and cutting the verbosity gap 43%.
  • Design choices are ablated with a mechanism rather than asserted: GRPO over SFT/DPO at fixed compute with measured KL drift (< 0.24 vs 0.43), trace coverage shown monotonic across three sampling schemes, and temperature annealing justified by measured KL evolution (0.37 → 0.19 → 0.11 as T goes 1.0 → 0.4 → 0.0).
  • Operationally label-free: the reward's correctness term is exact-string agreement with the debate consensus, so no dataset labels and no external judge enter training; the generalization evidence (symmetric GSM8K↔GSM-Plus transfer, ≥7B losing ≤0.2 pts on any transfer task) is the paper's case that this is real reasoning gain, not overfitting.

Limitations

  • Catastrophic forgetting under iteration, which the paper's own Limitations concedes it cannot eliminate ('potential model collapse' below 3B): at T=1.0, Round 2 loses 2.0 pts on GSM8K and gains only 13.5 pts on GSM-Plus, and Figure 2 shows Qwen-1.5B giving back 0.9 pts on GSM8K and 2.8 on GSM-Plus; Llama-3B at temperature 0.8 and 10K steps collapses to 2.73% on GSM8K and to 35.63 / 23.02 after two rounds. Temperature annealing 0.7 → 0.3 for ≤3B is a mitigation, not a fix — at T=0.4 Round-2 GSM-Plus is still 0.9 pts below Round 1.
  • The ceiling is the debate, not the data: the paper's Limitations says efficacy 'may degrade if debates are of poor quality or if initial agent performance is weak', and one round captures nearly all available gain — Round 2 adds at most +0.8 pts for ≥7B and more often loses 0.4–1.4 (mean forgetting Fgt2 0.92 pts ≥7B, 1.6 for smaller).
  • Scope is structured reasoning only (math, science MC-QA, commonsense); the paper flags open-ended generation and dialogue as unstudied, and states DTE still costs more to train than plain single-model fine-tuning.
  • Consensus errors propagate: correctness is agreement with the debate's majority answer, so a confident wrong consensus is a wrong training target — visible in Table 1 where Qwen-7B loses 2.35 pts on GSM8K and Qwen-7B / 1.5B lose on ARC-Challenge, with the paper attributing Round-1 Qwen-7B's GSM8K drop to traces that over-emphasised shortcut heuristics.
