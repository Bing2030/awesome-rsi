EvolveR: Self-Evolving LLM Agents through an Experience-Driven Lifecycle

Rong Wu, Xiaoman Wang, Jianbiao Mei, Pinlong Cai, Daocheng Fu, Cheng Yang, Licheng Wen, Xuemeng Yang, Yufan Shen, Yuxin Wang, Botian Shi

ICML 2026 · 2026

Local PDFOnlineCode

TL;DR

EvolveR closes the experience loop inside RL training: the agent distills its own trajectories into guiding/cautionary principles kept in a scored, deduplicated, pruned base, retrieves them via a learned <search experience> action, and GRPO-trains on experience-conditioned rollouts — beating Search-R1 across seven QA benchmarks.

Abstract

Current Large Language Model (LLM) agents show strong performance in tool use, but lack the crucial capability to systematically learn from their own experiences. While existing frameworks mainly focus on mitigating external knowledge gaps, they fail to address a more fundamental limitation: the inability to iteratively refine problem-solving strategies. In this work, we introduce EvolveR, a framework designed to enable agent to self-improve through a complete, closed-loop experience lifecycle. This lifecycle comprises two key stages: (1) Offline Self-Distillation, where the agent's interaction trajectories are synthesized into a structured repository of abstract, reusable strategic principles; (2) Online Interaction, where the agent interacts with tasks and actively retrieves distilled principles to guide its decision-making, accumulating a diverse set of behavioral trajectories. This loop employs a policy reinforcement mechanism to iteratively update the agent based on its performance. We demonstrate the effectiveness of EvolveR on complex multi-hop question-answering benchmarks, where it achieves superior performance over strong agentic baselines. Our work presents a comprehensive blueprint for agents that learn not only from external data but also from the consequences of their own actions, paving the way for more autonomous and continuously improving systems. Code is available at https://github.com/Edaizi/EvolveR.

Method

Three alternating phases (§3). (1) Offline self-distillation, policy frozen: the agent's own πθ reviews each trajectory under an expert persona and emits a principle — a natural-language description plus knowledge triples, typed 'guiding' (success) or 'cautionary' (failure). Same-problem candidates are pairwise-deduped by LLM votes; each survivor then passes a two-stage gate (BGE-M3 cosine retrieval + binary LLM equivalence check): add if max similarity < 0.85, else merge the source trajectory under the matched principle. Every principle carries a Laplace-smoothed trust score s(p)=(c_succ+1)/(c_use+2); scores below 0.3 are periodically pruned. (2) Online interaction: top-3 principles are retrieved on demand via a learned <search experience> action, alongside <search knowledge> document retrieval. (3) Policy evolution: GRPO (G=8 rollouts per prompt, 128 prompts/step) maximizes 1.0·exact-match + 0.1·format reward over experience-conditioned trajectories, after a ~700-sample GPT-4o cold-start SFT.

Key results

  • Seven QA benchmarks, EM: top average at both scales — 0.382 (Qwen2.5-3B) and 0.417 (7B) vs Search-R1 0.303 (base, 3B) / 0.385 (instruct, 7B); best in five of seven columns at 3B (NQ 0.434 vs 0.406, HotpotQA 0.373 vs 0.284, Bamboogle 0.328 vs 0.264), trailing Search-R1-base by 0.003 on TriviaQA (0.584 vs 0.587) and PopQA (0.434 vs 0.435).
  • Self- vs teacher-distillation reverses with scale (Table 2): GPT-4o-mini distillation wins at 0.5B (avg 0.220 vs 0.150) and 1.5B (0.290 vs 0.270), but self-distillation wins at 3B (0.382 vs 0.370) — the paper's 'cognitive alignment' claim.
  • Retrieval is load-bearing: the same trained weights denied the experience base at eval drop 0.382 → 0.340 (3B), 0.270 → 0.123 (1.5B), 0.150 → 0.078 (0.5B); swapping GRPO for SFT on the identical successful trajectories costs 0.382 → 0.357 (7% relative).
  • Quality control demonstrably works (manual audit of 100 principles): the high-score tier holds 82% ideal / 8% incorrect-misleading vs 26% / 24% in the low tier; the base grows to 49,934 principles with top-3 latency of 0.20 s and stable EM, peaking at 0.410 near 45k.
  • Scaling and learning dynamics: avg EM rises 0.150 (0.5B) → 0.270 (1.5B) → 0.382 (3B); principles distilled later score higher (interval-4 avg trust 0.500 vs 0.462 for interval 1), and pruning is threshold-robust (avg 0.265–0.276 for θprune ∈ {0.1, 0.3, 0.7, 0.9} on 1.5B).

Key advantages

  • Closes the loop inside RL rather than around a frozen model: distill every step (params frozen), retrieve during rollouts, GRPO over experience-conditioned trajectories — Reflexion/ExpeL/Memento never touch weights, Search-R1-style search RL has no experience channel. Component attribution: RL alone 0.325, retrieval without RL 0.357, full framework 0.382.
  • Memory with an audited trust ledger: the (c_succ+1)/(c_use+2) score decides survival (θprune = 0.3) and tracks human-judged quality (82% vs 26% ideal), robustly — performance moves only 0.265 → 0.276 across the whole θprune sweep.
  • Shows self-distillation can beat a stronger teacher at 3B: principles matched to the policy's own failure modes outperform GPT-4o-mini's, direct evidence against the external-scribing paradigm (ExpeL-style insights from a big model).
  • The experience base scales as an artifact: ~50k principles with 0.04–0.20 s top-3 retrieval (Milvus, BGE-M3) and no collapse under noise accumulation; full 3B lifecycle trains in ~39.4 h on 8×A100.

Limitations

  • Scale-dependent distiller (paper's own finding): below 3B, self-distillation loses to GPT-4o-mini teacher distillation (0.5B avg 0.150 vs 0.220; 1.5B 0.270 vs 0.290); the conclusion concedes principle quality is 'inherently tied to the base model's capabilities'.
  • Capability is split between policy and base, not internalized: denying retrieval at eval costs 0.382 → 0.340 (3B) and 0.150 → 0.078 (0.5B), and the w/ exp-absorb attempt to learn from retrieved principle tokens degrades 0.382 → 0.371 (noisy top-k), left to future work.
  • Distillation failure mode is vagueness: low-score-tier principles are 50% vague and 24% incorrect/misleading (audit of just 100 samples) — scoring prunes but never eliminates bad advice.
  • Scope is QA-shaped: seven knowledge-intensive QA benchmarks with an exact-match headline; whether strategy principles transfer to non-QA agent domains is untested.
