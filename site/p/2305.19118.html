PAPER · stage 6 · Multi-Agent Self-Improvement

Encouraging Divergent Thinking in Large Language Models through Multi-Agent Debate

Tian Liang, Zhiwei He, Wenxiang Jiao, Xing Wang, Yan Wang, Rui Wang, Yujiu Yang, Shuming Shi, Zhaopeng Tu

EMNLP 2024 · 2024

Local PDFOnline

TL;DR

Names and measures the Degeneration-of-Thought (DoT) failure of single-agent self-reflection — confidence locks in wrong answers — and fixes it with Multi-Agent Debate (MAD): two 'tit-for-tat' debaters plus a judge with adaptive break, lifting GPT-3.5-Turbo past GPT-4 on commonsense MT.

Abstract

Modern large language models (LLMs) like ChatGPT have shown remarkable performance on general language tasks but still struggle on complex reasoning tasks, which drives the research on cognitive behaviors of LLMs to explore human-like problem-solving strategies. Along this direction, one representative strategy is self-reflection, which asks an LLM to refine the solution with the feedback generated by itself iteratively. However, our study shows that such reflection-style methods suffer from the Degeneration-of-Thought (DoT) problem: once the LLM has established confidence in its solutions, it is unable to generate novel thoughts later through reflection even if its initial stance is incorrect. To address the DoT problem, we propose a Multi-Agent Debate (MAD) framework, in which multiple agents express their arguments in the state of "tit for tat" and a judge manages the debate process to obtain a final solution. Clearly, our MAD framework encourages divergent thinking in LLMs which would be helpful for tasks that require deep levels of contemplation. Experiment results on two challenging datasets, commonsense machine translation and counter-intuitive arithmetic reasoning, demonstrate the effectiveness of our MAD framework. Extensive analyses suggest that the adaptive break of debate and the modest level of "tit for tat" state are required for MAD to obtain good performance. Moreover, we find that LLMs might not be a fair judge if different LLMs are used for agents. Code is available at https://github.com/Skytliang/Multi-Agents-Debate.

Method

The paper first names DoT: once an LLM is confident in an answer, self-reflection stops generating novel thoughts even when the initial stance is wrong — operationalized as the percentage of opposition between adjacent iterations over 5 forced rounds (Fig. 1): self-reflection's disagreement stays low (it re-affirms the wrong CoT answer) while debate keeps it high. MAD then runs three agents: an affirmative debater, a negative debater prompted to 'disagree with the affirmative side's points', and a judge. Debaters speak in fixed order, each conditioned on the full debate history H. The judge has two modes: discriminative (is a correct solution on the table? True ends the debate early — the adaptive break) and extractive (pull the final answer from H once the iteration limit — 3 in experiments — is hit). All roles share one backbone LLM (vicuna-7b/13b-v1.5-16k, GPT-3.5-Turbo-0301, GPT-4-0314), zero-shot at temperature 0. Testbeds: Common MT (Chinese⇒English commonsense ambiguity; 200 lexical / 450 contextless / 350 contextual) and the authors' 200-question Counter-Intuitive AR set.

Key results

  • Common MT human direct assessment (1-5), GPT-3.5-Turbo baseline → +MAD vs GPT-4: lexical 3.14 → 3.78 (GPT-4 3.41), contextless 3.43 → 3.67 (3.63), contextual 3.57 → 3.67 (3.65) — MAD on Turbo beats GPT-4 on all three, and on COMET/BLEURT it matches or edges GPT-4 (e.g. contextual COMET 85.3 vs 85.0); Self-Reflect reaches only 3.43 lexical
  • Counter-Intuitive AR accuracy: 26.0% (Turbo) → 37.0% with MAD, vs Self-Reflect 27.5%, CoT 28.0%, Self-Consistency 29.5%; GPT-4 stays ahead at 51.0%
  • DoT mitigation vs Self-Reflect: human-labeled ambiguity-error (bias) 29.0 → 24.8 and output diversity (100 - Self-BLEU) 19.3 → 49.7
  • Math/symbolic transfer (Turbo, Appendix C): MAD vs CoT — GSM8K 73.8 vs 70.2, AddSub 92.1 vs 87.3, BBH Date 65.2 vs 56.4, Penguin 63.7 vs 58.9; cost 2.46x CoT's generated tokens (Self-Reflect 1.83x)
  • Judge behavior: debater quality dominates judge quality (Turbo debaters score 3.47 HUMAN with a vicuna-13b judge vs 3.69 with a Turbo judge); with mixed backbones the judge favors its own LLM — GPT-4 judging GPT-4-vs-Turbo picks the GPT-4 side 120/200 vs 77 (same-LLM rows instead favor the negative side, 104 vs 87)
  • Scaling ablations: more debaters hurt (2 → 3 → 4 debaters: COMET 84.4 → 83.1 → 82.9) because debaters 'forget' earlier views in long histories; the modest level-2 'tit for tat' beats both full consensus (3.00 → 3.81 HUMAN) and forced total disagreement (disagreement 0.988, 3.72)

Key advantages

  • First to name and operationally measure DoT (adjacent-iteration disagreement), turning 'self-reflection plateaus' into a diagnosable failure with a causal account — bias, rigidity, missing external feedback — each mapped to a mechanism of the fix
  • Divergence by construction rather than hope: the negative debater is instructed to oppose, so the loop cannot quietly agree with itself the way Self-Reflect does (diversity 49.7 vs 19.3)
  • The judge's adaptive break ends most debates after one round and outperforms forcing any fixed iteration count (Fig. 5), keeping overhead at 2.46x CoT tokens
  • Reports the negative results later debate work leans on: adding debaters hurts, and an LLM judge is partial to its own backbone — so use one LLM for all roles

Limitations

  • Time/compute cost of multi-round interaction: 2.46x CoT's generated tokens on Counter-Intuitive AR, vs 1.83x for Self-Reflect (Sec. Limitations, Appendix E)
  • Long-context degradation: with more debaters, 'LLM-based debaters tend to forget the views of other debaters' and judge extraction degrades — 2 debaters beat 3 and 4 (Table 7)
  • LLM judges are not impartial across backbones (self-preference, Table 6); the authors recommend identical LLMs for judge and debaters
  • Counter-Intuitive AR is the authors' own 200-question set (elicitation questionnaires + web puzzles), and MAD does not close the gap to GPT-4 there (37.0 vs 51.0)
